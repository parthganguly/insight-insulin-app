# Predicting the insulin difference between chickpea preparations

**The amount of starch released after 20 minutes distinguished two chickpea porridges that an earlier human reference treated alike. Cooking duration almost matched the assay prediction. Neither the porridge effect nor the earlier reference transferred reliably to a coarse-versus-fine bread contrast.** This supports measuring a specified prepared food for research. It does not establish a general milling adjustment, a required app input, or an absolute insulin score. Decision D remains open.

## Equal nutrients, different two-hour responses

Cai et al. randomized ten adults to three chickpea porridges on successive mornings during a four-day inpatient visit with a duodenal sampling tube. Each meal supplied 29.5 g starch, 11 g protein, 6.6 g fibre and 3.7 g fat. The broken cells were disrupted before heating for 28 minutes. The separated intact cells cooked for 80 minutes. Coarsely milled clusters cooked for 50 minutes. Water served alongside the meals compensated for differences in preparation water. This design separates the predictions of equal starch and a single intact-cell category from measurements on the food as served. Duration, particle size and cellular structure vary together. [Cai paper and supplement](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42255-025-01309-7)

The released insulin series allowed two independent reductions of all ten complete crossover participants. Each used the seven published points from 0 to 120 minutes, an individual time-zero baseline and linearly interpolated baseline crossings. We integrated the positive area for each person before averaging. The protocol lists an additional predose draw at −10 minutes. The released figure source supplies one time-zero value per arm and person. The relationship of that value to the earlier draw and the precise first-bite offset are undocumented here. The first-peak geometric means and signed mean curves remain separate. [Cai et al., Figure 2 source data](https://pmc-oa-opendata.s3.amazonaws.com/PMC12286859.1/42255_2025_1309_MOESM4_ESM.xlsx)

|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Broken | 1,983 ± 263 | 1.000 | 1.000 | 1.000 |
| Separated cells | 1,445 ± 226 | 0.728 | 0.732 | 0.706 |
| Clusters | 564 ± 102 | 0.285 | 0.452 | 0.706 |

The primary comparison was the average absolute log error on the two broken-referenced ratios. It was **0.233 for the 20-minute assay**, 0.470 for the earlier human reference and 0.787 for equal starch. Any prediction assigning the same multiplier to both intact arms has error at least 0.470 on these two means. This follows from half their absolute log separation. It gives no bound on models that can distinguish the two preparations. The 60-, 90- and 120-minute assays scored 0.456, 0.373 and 0.391. The source response ratio came from the signed 0–120 mean curves for intact and disrupted hummus in 15 other participants. Starch was matched at 26.8 g in the donor, while serving size, energy and other characteristics differed. The donor shares investigators with Cai. [Alshaalan et al., Figures 4 and 7](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.foodres.2024.114517)

The cluster/separated ratio was **0.391**. A paired normal-theory Fieller interval was 0.254–0.572 (nominal 95%, nine degrees of freedom). The paired mean difference was −880 ± 194 SEM µU·min/mL. Signed area gave 0.374 and clipping only the observed sample nodes gave 0.404. The actual-meal assay predicted **0.616**, from 5.33 versus 8.64 g starch hydrolysed at 20 minutes. The assay still underestimated the size of the reduction. It nearly matched separated versus broken but overpredicted clusters versus broken. The interval describes sampling uncertainty under the specified model. It excludes graph-reading and assay uncertainty and is not an interval for individuals or for a model's accuracy.

Information choice mattered as much as calibration. Anchoring the separated arm to the hummus reference and using the assay for clusters gave primary error 0.228. A power fitted to the single hummus contrast worsened it to 0.645. After seeing the Cai outcomes, using the ratio of the two intact cooking times, 50/80, predicted **0.625** for clusters versus separated cells. With the same hummus anchor its error was 0.235. Applying time alone to all three arms predicted the wrong direction against broken (error 1.601). Thus the assay beat the fixed category rule, but its small advantage over a more specific, post hoc process rule does not justify requiring a measurement. The assay is pancreatic amylolysis of the actual preparation, with its own protocol. It is not interchangeable with an ingredient's total starch or a generic rapid-digestibility label.

Absolute levels answer a different question. A post-result check carried the disrupted hummus signed area of 2,443 µU·min/mL across without target insulin calibration. Three-arm mean absolute error was 1,112 for the common donor, 634 for the human process reference and 448 for the local 20-minute assay. Proportionally increasing the donor level for the larger starch dose worsened assay error to 627. These are empirical forecasts using another cohort's insulin and a different area convention. The contrasts are the stronger finding. [Numerical results](pilot/results.json) · [Absolute levels](pilot/levels.json)

## Does the result travel to bread?

**Documented substitution within one research program.** We linked Bajka's earlier baked-roll recipe and digestion experiment to a later clinical study of rolls with 0%, 30% and 60% cellular chickpea ingredient. Those are flour substitution labels. They are not percentages of served starch. The earlier recipe made 12 rolls per batch. The later supplement gives fixed 0–120-minute arithmetic insulin areas of 4,523, 4,189 and 2,540 µU·min/mL. Its variable first-peak result uses a different endpoint. We could not establish the exact positive-clipping convention for the fixed window, identical batches, the paired covariance or identical subsets across arms (reported n=20/20/19). [Earlier assay and recipes](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7859705/) · [Later clinical study and supplement](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10131617/)

Compared with the white roll, early starch release from Figure 3A gave mean absolute log error **0.111**, versus 0.318 for wheat flour grams and 0.327 for equality. For the *60% versus 30%* contrast, however, the observed ratio was 0.606. The same assay predicted 0.757 (error 0.222), while wheat flour predicted 0.509 (error 0.174). Using the published fitted digestion curve instead of measured markers predicted approximately 0.659 and reversed that ranking. Including bread sugars also changed performance. The earlier assay and later insulin summary were measured separately; the coordinator encountered the later outcomes while acquiring the supplement. This is an outcome-exposed recipe transfer. [Predictions and graph-reading bounds](bread/results.json)

**Independent laboratory and recipe.** Kanata et al. compared two breads with 30% coarse or fine chickpea flour by flour weight. Both supplied 50 g calculated available carbohydrate and used the same baking temperature and time. Mixing time, serving mass, fibre and measured starch differed. The amount of chickpea starch in the serving, retained plant-cell morphology after baking and early starch release from the final breads were unavailable. The larger bread-crumb cells characterized air pores. [Kanata et al., Tables 1–2 and Figure 4b](https://doi.org/10.1039/D5FO00348B)

A frozen, conditional calculation assigned 30% of starch to chickpeas and used the cluster/broken porridge ratio for that share. It predicted **0.785** for coarse versus fine bread. Using the porridge assay gave **0.835**. The signed two-hour arm-mean ratio was approximately **1.026**, and the reported positive three-hour area ratio was **1.021**. Equality was closer than either transferred forecast at both central readings. Adjusting for measured total starch lowered the forecasts further. Under ±2-pixel independent reading variation the signed two-hour ratio spans 0.955–1.101; this is a graph-reading envelope, not a sampling interval. The study does not establish equality. A same-bread assay had zero coverage, so its predictive performance on this pair remains unknown. [Conditional forecasts and separate outcomes](external/results.json)

## How much do the assay readings matter?

Opus tested the 20-minute readings against two illustrative perturbations. The separation between the assay and duration forecasts was 0.014 on the log scale, about 0.08 of the observed paired log-ratio standard error. Both forecasts share the same reference for separated cells in the anchored comparison. At this precision they provide little basis for choosing which input INSIGHT should collect.

At the wider perturbation, varying all three 20-minute readings independently by up to **3.478 percentage points** gave an assay error from **0.082 to 0.469** against the two broken-referenced ratios. Even the maximum was just below the category reference's 0.470. The advantage disappears at a half-width of about **3.498 points**. These widths come from dips in two plotted late digestion means. They are sensitivity tests, not estimates of 20-minute assay precision. Moving the cluster reading alone by 1.30 points would bring its prediction to the upper limit of the paired clinical interval. The Figure 1d points are means of three determinations, and the authors show a nonlinear fit with SEM. Individual 20-minute assay replicates or covariance would resolve a different uncertainty than paired clinical sampling. ## Decision and next discriminator

Keep early digestion of the actual food, specific preparation, and the cheaper empirical references as competing research inputs. The earlier rice-lunch preparation contrast and earlier dose/reference gains survive alongside their calibration failures. A record saying only “coarse” or “intact” cannot earn a transported insulin adjustment from these results. The next useful contrast is one where documented cooking duration and measured early release make distinguishable predictions after portion and recipe are accounted for. A preparation variant with independently measured release and an existing fixed-window human response would suffice. Different duration at similar release, or different release at similar duration, would help decide whether acquiring an assay earns its cost. This checkpoint supports neither individual forecasting nor a general consumer score.
