# Kanata external bread contrast

Kanata and colleagues, 2025. [Original paper, Figure 4b and Tables 1–2](https://doi.org/10.1039/D5FO00348B). The paper calls the insulin graph Figure 3b in prose but the graph is labelled Figure 4b. The [archived figure from the publication](https://www.researchgate.net/figure/Postprandial-responses-and-the-iAUC-for-a-glucose-b-insulin-and-c-GLP-1-p-005_fig3_391879497) is licensed CC BY 3.0. kanata-screenshot.png preserves the displayed original; kanata-B.png isolates the insulin panel. The original publisher file requests returned HTTP 403. Run kanata-extract.py to reproduce the local aggregate CSV.

Each test serving contained 50 g of calculated available carbohydrate, made with 42% refined wheat flour, 28% wholemeal wheat flour, and 30% chickpea flour by flour weight. Fine chickpea particles passed a 210 µm sieve; the coarse flour was roller-milled from dehulled chickpeas. Coarse flour was a size distribution; about 62% was above 1.45 mm. Coarse dough mixed for 12 minutes versus 10 minutes for fine dough; both breads baked at 190°C for 18–20 min, then underwent the same freezing and overnight thaw. Coarse vs fine had 39.7 vs 41.8 g measured total starch, 15.5 vs 14.5 g protein, and 11.7 vs 8.01 g fibre. Different serving sizes (136 vs 126 g) supplied 1305 vs 1215 kJ.

There was no rate assay on these served breads and no source-specific chickpea starch mass. Plant cell morphology in the finished bread was hypothesized from flour processing. The crumb-cell results describe air pores. A model using 30% flour weight to attenuate a source contrast must flag it as an assumed proxy for the chickpea contribution; the actual mass fraction of starch and fraction of insulin exposure attributable to chickpeas are unknown.

Fifteen healthy adults completed all conditions. The paper measured fasting, 30, 45, 60, 90, 120, and 180 min serum insulin concentrations. The 120- and 180-min signed net areas are derived by trapezoidal integration of the arm means relative to their own plotted baseline. The published bars are mean positive-only iAUC to 180 min. Plot resolution limits precision, primarily where markers overlap at baseline; differences between ratios near unity should not be overinterpreted. No significant insulin differences were found and the study was powered for glucose. The precision of the within-person ratio cannot be recovered from the displayed marginal data. No participant-level data were used.
