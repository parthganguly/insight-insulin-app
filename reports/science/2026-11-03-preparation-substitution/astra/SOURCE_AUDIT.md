# Independent staple comparison acquisition

## Executable result

The optional `python extract.py` source audit expects the local publisher-text extraction and indexed abstract under raw/. The portable runner uses the retained factual recipe rows and graph readings. It parses the 26 recipe/total rows from the archived primary publication, verifies three reported 180-minute insulin means and SEMs against the independently acquired indexed primary abstract, and reconstructs rounded 120-minute insulin means and SEMs from documented readings of original Figure 2. Outputs are schafer_recipe.csv and schafer_aggregate.json. The raw files are archive material, including the figure obtained through an image delivery proxy from the publisher. No participant-level data were transcribed.

Schäfer et al. randomized meal order in nine diet-treated adults with type 2 diabetes. The three lunches were a week apart, contained yellow dried peas, Granola potatoes or a mixture, and were prepared with different accompaniments for the potato-only meal. Serum insulin was measured after two baseline samples and at 30-minute intervals through 180 minutes. The principal source for the arms, component energy and carbohydrate is Table 1. In terms of the reported composition-table staple carbohydrate, the pea fractions are 1, 0.6767 and 0. The authors call the blend two-thirds pea. Total meal carbohydrates were 40.6, 40.0 and 41.6 g. Inspecting ingredient weights or subtracting fiber from the reported carbohydrate column produces a different operation. The study does not report a laboratory assay of available carbohydrate.

Figure 2 (lower panel) shows approximate 120-minute insulin iAUCs of 8.3, 11.0 and 22.1 nmol min/L. Treatment letters are a, ab and b. The directly reported potato/pea ratio is 2.7 (p<0.05). At 180 min, insulin means were 13.8, 15.4 and 31.2, with SEMs 4.3, 3.9 and 6.9 and p=0.0514. Rounded 120-min readings are accurate to the scale of a few tenths; retain the authors’ reported ratio when higher precision is unnecessary. The paper supplies no contrast covariance for a paired interval. This is a mixed-meal experiment in T2D with a different pulse and changes in meat, cream, butter, and vegetables. It can test a near-neighbor transfer question at the recipe level. It cannot certify a lentil-specific effect.

## Same-staple candidates

- Bornet et al. (1987) randomized three challenges per person, but only three patients tested each starch, and each person tested a single type of starch. The 180-minute glucose-reference insulinemic index during mixed meals was 103±37 for lentils, 126±16 for rice, and 274±164 for potato (reported means±SEM). For foods alone, means were approximately 95% aside from bread and the between-food difference was nonsignificant. The cross-staple comparisons use different patients, and the endpoint is a reference-normalized percentage. Figures 2 and 4 provide the group size and endpoint. [Primary full text](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/19614236_Insulinemic_and_glycemic_indexes_of_six_starch-rich_foods_taken_alone_and_in_a_mixed_meal_by_type_2_diabetics)
- Coulston et al. (1984) tested potato, rice, spaghetti and lentils as the main source of carbohydrate (66% of meal carbohydrate) in matched-macronutrient mixed meals. The abstract describes similar glucose responses with lentil and rice, higher with potato, and says insulin followed a parallel pattern. Precise insulin means and time windows were unavailable in the accessible publisher preview. [Primary record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/6388305/)
- Krezowski et al. (1987) tested 50 g carbohydrate from potato, rice, lentil and other starches in type 2 diabetes, recorded insulin for five hours, and used within-person controls. The accessible primary abstract reports different insulin/glucose relationships among foods. It supplies no numeric target arm contrast, so the secondary-extracted lentil/bread values were not taken as an estimate for the potato or rice head-to-head. [Primary record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3556106/)
- NCT02940158 tests two lentil serving sizes and both white rice and white potato in healthy adults; the open registration hasResults=false. This identifies a design without supplying public-registry numerical outcomes. [Registry](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT02940158)

## Direct healthy-adult potato/lentil transfer

Collier, McLean & O'Dea randomized the order of six test meals in seven healthy adults after overnight fasting. The four arms without IV glucose are relevant here. The paper describes 75 g carbohydrate as either 438 g potato or 121 g lentils. Fat arms added 37.5 g fat as 46.5 g butter to the same staple. The methods report the carbohydrate quantity but not an available-carbohydrate assay protocol. The plotted insulin means at baseline and 15, 30, 60, 90, 120, 180 and 240 minutes appear in Figure 1 (p. 51).

Across the curves, the reported three-way ANOVA favors lower insulin after lentils (p<0.01). The published incremental areas, in order potato, potato + fat, lentil, lentil + fat, are 3333±590, 3024±452, 1796±333 and 1324±256 mU min/L. The paragraph does not identify its integration window. The authors' insulin-to-glucose area ratios are another endpoint.

The zoomed original graph supports a separate, descriptive calculation over precisely 0–120 minutes. Run collier_fig1.py to check the PDF hash and perform calibrated readings and trapezoidal integration using each mean curve's own fasting intercept. The ratio of lentil to potato is approximately 0.54 without added butter fat and 0.41 with butter fat. Varying baseline by ±2 mU/L and other marker readings by ±1 mU/L in opposite directions gives sensitivity ranges of 0.395–0.725 and 0.248–0.616, respectively. Their breadth reflects duplicated/overlapping markers and partly obscured fasting intercepts. These extrema describe digitization assumptions, not statistical uncertainty. No paired-contrast confidence interval or p value for this two-hour reconstruction can be derived. The published ANOVA applies to the complete curves. Two contrasts share seven participants and are not independent replications.
