# How far does a staple substitution forecast travel?

**Cooking changed the prediction in a way that the staple name and exchange fraction could not capture.** In Wei's crossover the rice serving, potato serving and other lunch ingredients were identical in the two potato arms. Holding the potato at pressure for 30 seconds instead of 20 minutes gave a hard/soft ratio of **0.671** for the reported mean positive two-hour insulin area. A rule using staple identity and amount alone necessarily predicts **1.000** for this pair. An assay of the prepared starch predicted **0.705**. A provisional rule using only the cooking description predicted **0.667**, but it was chosen after seeing these outcomes. This comparison supports recording preparation for research. It does not establish a need to assay every serving. [Wei et al., methods and Tables 1, 3–5 and 9](https://doi.org/10.3390/nu18060973)

## Testing the earlier lentil forecasts

Opus froze the fixed potato reference **0.631**, the fraction-scaled forecast **0.398** at full exchange, and a linear-in-share alternative **0.261** before inspecting new target insulin estimates. All are lentil/potato ratios. The donors were geometric mean two-hour insulin areas from a 50 g available-carbohydrate meal with half its carbohydrate exchanged. Equality, proportional carbohydrate and pooled-donor forecasts were retained. The new direct comparisons do not report two-hour geometric means on that operator, so the prespecified primary panel has **zero eligible contrasts**. The following panels answer narrower questions. [Freeze, alternatives and thresholds](public/model/PLAN.md) · [Earlier donor study](https://doi.org/10.1093/jn/nxy018)

* **Direct healthy crossover, with and without butter.** Seven people received 75 g carbohydrate from lentils or potato. In the no-butter meals, the original mean curves gave a two-hour signed area of approximately 1,726 versus 3,183 mU·min/L, a ratio of **0.542**. The fixed rule was closer than the scaled rule. With 37.5 g fat added as butter to each meal, the estimates were 1,075 versus 2,632, a ratio of **0.408**; the scaled rule was closer. The pooled scaled comparator (.570) did particularly well without butter. The observations lie on opposite sides of the fixed/scaled error midpoint of .501. Baselines are partly obscured in the graph; the two reading-sensitivity intervals, .395–.725 and .248–.616, both cross that midpoint. The study reports a difference across the complete potato and lentil curves. It supplies no paired uncertainty for the reconstructed two-hour change in contrast with butter. The paper's directly printed area ratios, .539 and .438, remain separate: observation continued to four hours and the area paragraph does not restate its window. Treating the named staple as a full exchange assumes the paper's 75 g carbohydrate corresponds to a comparable available dose. Mean-curve integration does not recover a geometric mean or mean participant-positive area. [Collier et al., Figure 1 and p. 52](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF00252263) · [Coordinates and area readings](astra/collier_chart_120.csv)
* **Independent check of what a shared full replacement implies.** At fixed available carbohydrate, if the final lentil meal is identical, dividing lentil/potato by lentil/rice cancels the lentil response. This gives a testable rice/potato ratio of **0.698 fixed** or **0.487 scaled**. A simpler forecast using the earlier rice and potato control areas directly gives **0.548**. In nine healthy participants eating 50 g available carbohydrate as freshly boiled polished rice or reconstituted instant mash, the medians of their individual positive two-hour areas were 9 and 20 nmol·min/L. The ratio was **0.450**. Absolute log errors were .439 fixed, .079 scaled, .197 direct and .799 for equality. The ratio of mean individual glucose-normalized insulin indexes was **60/128 = .469**, with the same ranking. These are two summaries of one cohort; neither is a geometric mean. This test supports the implied difference between the two plain staples, without identifying either lentil response or a mixture law. [Lan-Pidhainy thesis, chapter 5](https://www.collectionscanada.gc.ca/obj/thesescanada/vol2/OTU/TC-OTU-31822.pdf) · [Journal report](https://doi.org/10.1038/ejcn.2011.28)
* **Bread and other pulses test coverage.** In Thorburn's study the published three-hour ratios were .499 for lentils versus white bread without added salt and .399 with added salt. Approximate signed two-hour mean-curve ratios were .33 and .29. Each was below even the pooled scaled forecast of .570. A wheat-specific extension using the earlier muffin gives .846 fixed and about .741 scaled if all control carbohydrate is exchanged. The food itself supplied 50 g carbohydrate, but both meals also contained milk of unspecified carbohydrate content, so full whole-meal exchange is only a scenario. Five participants were shared across the two six-person food groups. Curve-based three-hour estimates did not fully reproduce the printed areas; the scan, operator and separate readings are preserved. A second study in nine people with type 2 diabetes reported a potato/pea 120-minute area ratio of 2.7. Its blend lay between the pure arms and was not statistically separated from either. Its pulse species, sides and fraction definitions differ. These panels do not fill the primary lentil GM panel. [Thorburn et al., Figures 1–2 and Table I](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.292.6537.1697) · [Schäfer et al., Table 1 and Figure 2](https://doi.org/10.1093/ajcn/78.1.99)

## An intervention with participant-level two-hour areas

Wei et al. gave 20 healthy young women three lunches after a standard breakfast. Control was 90 g uncooked rice; each potato lunch had 60 g rice and 130 g potato before cooking, with the other ingredients held constant. The potato displaced 23.2 g of the rice's 69.5 g carbohydrate, or .292 of the whole meal's 79.5 g. These shares are distinct. The same pressure cooker and 0.75 cm slices gave core temperatures of 57.6°C and 77.4°C after the short and long holds. Pre-lunch and six post-lunch capillary plasma insulin draws extended to 120 minutes. The authors describe trapezoidal area above baseline and print means ± SEM. The precise implementation at crossings is undocumented. [Original trial](https://doi.org/10.3390/nu18060973)

**What the newer assay bought.** We mapped short/long microwave treatments in the earlier Zhao paper to short/long pressure cooking. Their equipment, portions, and samples differed. Food-specific potato RDS fractions from the earlier figure were about 16% and 44%; the trial preparation assays gave 9.4% and 68.1%. Both calculations keep the same Wei rice fraction, so the earlier-template route saves the current *potato* assay only. Zhao Figure 1 bar values do not sum to 100 despite the axis caption. We normalized each duration's RDS/SDS/RS bar heights by their sum; the resulting rapid fractions agree with the independently plotted starch digestion index. The 2/6-minute durations map by increasing digestibility; Zhao's sentence listing the two preparation abbreviations is ambiguous in order. This is a process analogy, not a claim of matching batches. The local assay reduced hard/soft log error from **.221 to .050**, but scarcely changed error against rice (.110 to .109). With 10 g common nonstaple carbohydrate treated as fully rapid, local RDS forecasts .795/1.009 and the earlier template .818/.923; the pooled ratio-to-rice ordering changes. The RDS+SDS model has a different absolute-versus-contrast trade-off. For the cheaper cooking-only benchmark, treating soft potato as rice-like per gram of actual starch instead of per gram of carbohydrate changes the hard/soft forecast from .667 to .781. Neither extreme was frozen before the two outcomes. [Zhao et al., Figure 1 and Tables 1–2](https://doi.org/10.3390/nu12092759) · [All input scenarios and image-reading sensitivities](contrast/results.json)

**A near match from a different endpoint.** Zhao's ratio of standalone potato glycemic indexes, 56.2/82.9 = .678, looks close to Wei's .671. It compares glucose after potato alone, with no shared rice. In Zhao's concurrent potato–rice meals, the corresponding two-hour glucose areas were 213.5 and 229.3 against rice at 230.4. Transferring those actual meal ratios predicts .927 and .995 and a minimal/high ratio of .931. The difference in co-ingredients and the distinction between glucose and insulin matter.

**A limit on what reweighting a linear dose model could fix.** If rice contributes proportionally to its dose and the unchanged background and added potato both contribute nonnegative areas, retaining two-thirds of the rice imposes a minimum ratio of two-thirds. The hard meal was .625, about **966 mIU·min/L below that minimum** at the printed means. For the paired gap, the two published SEMs allow a gap SEM from 836 to 2,438 across all correlations. Even the most favorable correlation gives an absolute t statistic of only 1.15 for 19 degrees of freedom. The central point violates this structural restriction; a population violation is unestablished. The authors' significant hard-versus-soft result tests a different contrast. ## Decision and next discriminator

Retain substitution fraction and displaced staple for described preparations. A process-specific input can usefully separate meals that share those inputs. The evidence does not select a universal fraction coefficient, require a current assay, or support a consumer adjustment. The earlier fixed-reference primary failure, its favorable potato/rice contrast, the fraction-scaled development gain (stronger on potato than rice), and the earlier Oxford native-level gain remain intact. Haigh's inconsistent time windows remain unresolved.

The informative next observation would separate a process description from a current assay **before** scoring another human contrast: for example, two specified preparations with similar cooking labels but materially different measured rapid fractions, or different labels with similar fractions. No special trial design is required to compare frozen predictions. Useful acquisition fields are the served rice and potato weights, exact cook protocol, linked starch assay if available, and arithmetic means of participant-positive 0–120 areas under a declared baseline.
