# Same-laboratory transfer of four sugar responses

One healthy overweight cohort supplied both 75 g fasting donor contrasts. A separate impaired-glucose-tolerance (IGT) study supplied the evaluation. At two hours, protocol-matched references underestimated the relative T/G versus I/S contrast in both periods. The three-hour fasting double ratio transferred closely because both individual formulation ratios were underestimated by almost the same proportion.

Here T/G means the trehalose-to-glucose area ratio and I/S the isomaltulose-to-sucrose ratio. D = (T/G)/(I/S). Each quantity uses ratios of arithmetic group means. A common fractional response for both substitutions predicts D = 1. Signed trapezoids on group trajectories constitute a supporting endpoint. The requested arithmetic mean of individually positive two-hour incremental areas requires additional information.

## Primary sources and shared participants

Both donor reports give the same ten healthy overweight participants by sex distribution (8 men), age (31, SE 4 years), BMI (27.7, SE 0.8), fasting glucose (5.1, SE 0.1 mmol/L), and insulin (14, SE 1.9 µU/mL). The investigators’ joint 2008 abstract explicitly describes all four drinks in one randomized crossover of ten adults with these characteristics. This establishes one donor cohort. The 2009 papers each present two arms of that study and each gives a minimum one-week washout. [2008 conference abstract, p. S134, T3:PS.12](https://www.nature.com/articles/ijo200847.pdf) · [T/G, pp. 1396–1398](https://doi.org/10.1017/S000711450999050X) · [I/S, pp. 1409–1411](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0007114509990687)

The evaluation was a separately recruited sample of ten older adults (6 men, 4 women) with OGTT-confirmed IGT. Each received all four sugars with a week between visits. Van Can’s dissertation distinguishes Chapters 2A/B (healthy overweight) from Chapter 3 (IGT), and a separate 2009 conference report already describes the IGT cohort. The donor and evaluation records support separate studies by the same laboratory. Participant-level cross-study identifiers are unavailable. Repeated arms, windows, papers and abstract reports add no independent cohorts. The 2009 research was sponsored by Cargill. [IGT original, Methods and Table 1](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0007114511006714) · [2009 conference abstract, T3:PO.143](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6444784/) · [Author’s dissertation](https://doi.org/10.26481/dis.20121031jc)

## What was matched

All three original papers specify an overnight-fasted carbohydrate challenge in 400 mL of water, 75 g carbohydrate or carbohydrate equivalents, consumed within 15 minutes. They specify a same-day lunch given with the corresponding sugar. Both periods used venous plasma. Both 2009 reports name a LINCO human insulin radioimmunoassay. The IGT paper specifies a Linco double-antibody radioimmunoassay. No traceable equivalence of calibration lots or assay standards is reported. Figures use µU/mL. The nominal unit of the reconstructed area is µU·min/mL.

In both studies, lunch energy equalled half the participant’s calculated daily resting energy expenditure. Of total lunch energy, 55% came from carbohydrate, 30% from fat and 15% from protein. The beverage supplied 25% of total meal energy. Sugar gram amounts at lunch and the remaining meal ingredients were unavailable at arm level. All trials paired morning and lunch treatment, so the contrast includes current meal composition and prior sugar exposure together. Individualized doses, recruited cohort, baseline, and unrecorded aspects of meal formulation can affect transport. [Donor T/G Methods](https://doi.org/10.1017/S000711450999050X) · [Donor I/S Methods](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0007114509990687) · [IGT Methods](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0007114511006714)

We carried each measured pre-dose concentration forward to nominal zero for the primary signed calculation. Fasting baselines in T/G/I/S order were 12.78/15.29/14.97/14.12 µU/mL, lunch baselines 10.47/10.26/13.53/10.72. The target study gives an 08:45 breakfast.

Alternative operators include the full −5-to-end integral, interpolation of concentration on the −5-to-30 line at the clock origin, and morning windows measured literally from stated 08:40. Signed donor D at two hours ranges from 0.864 to 0.891 at breakfast and 0.862 to 0.869 at lunch under these choices. The pointwise SEMs in the original curves cannot give a paired ratio CI. Under an adversarial ±0.25 PDF-point perturbation per center with no calibration changes, the nominal donor D at 120 ranges from 0.798 to 0.936 at breakfast and 0.825 to 0.900 at lunch. These are reading sensitivities. The directly encoded vertices require no manual choice of center.

## Transfer results

The first six rows use signed group-mean integrals. “Common” is the geometric mean of the donor ratios assigned to both substitutions. All common factors predict D = 1. Reported three-hour areas are retained as a distinct operator. They were calculated by trapezoids with baseline subtraction in the 2009 papers. Neither donor paper specifies individual positive-only clipping. The IGT paper labels its three-hour results incremental trapezoidal areas and gives no explicit below-baseline rule.

At two hours the matched and common references have identical mean absolute component log error at breakfast (0.096). At lunch their errors are 0.200 and 0.126. Reusing the fasting donor for lunch gives component error 0.326, D error 0.397. The protocol-specific D change forecast (lunch D divided by breakfast D) is 0.998 versus observed 1.320, whereas the no-change forecast is 1.000. Three-hour fasting signed and published results agree on a useful window-specific double ratio, despite roughly 15% underprediction of each constituent ratio. The all-arm transfers underestimate every two-hour signed level. For T/G/I/S respectively the errors are −40/−30/−36/−33% at breakfast and −38/−24/−34/−45% at lunch.

An exploratory scan of duration gives a reason to treat the 180-minute agreement separately. On the interpolated fasting means, cumulative D crosses one near 76 minutes in the donor and 116 in IGT. During lunch the donor crosses near 58 minutes. The IGT lunch trace stays above one at all integer end times 1–180. These times approximate crossings of reconstructed group means. They are sensitive to the small number of samples, especially in the first 30 minutes.

## Repeated lunch values conflict with raw area

The donor papers print the identical afternoon insulin areas 10,693 and 14,634 for T/G and I/S. All four fasting native areas are within 0.2% of the literal signed −5 to +180 integrations. At lunch, literal integrals give T/G 10,470/14,809 and I/S 7,655/9,378. The T/G closeness cannot establish which source values are correct. The separate I/S table in the author’s later dissertation also repeats 10,693/14,634. [Donor T/G Table 1, p. 1398](https://doi.org/10.1017/S000711450999050X) · [Donor I/S Table 1, p. 1411](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0007114509990687) · [Dissertation, Chapter 2B, Table 1, p. 59](https://doi.org/10.26481/dis.20121031jc)

For any nonnegative concentration and baseline, the positive part of the baseline-subtracted concentration cannot exceed the raw concentration. Under common complete records and linear segments this gives a check independent of paired covariance or baseline selection. Even including the extra five minutes before lunch, the plotted absolute raw I and S integrals are only 10,158 and 11,361. The printed incremental values exceed those ceilings by 535 and 3,273. Neither positive clipping nor an alternate nonnegative baseline reconciles the results on this measurement grid. The table is preserved with the discrepancy. Its apparent lunch equality is unsafe evidence for any rule. The author-reported paired tests were P<0.01 for T/G in both periods, P=0.03 for fasting I/S and P<0.01 for lunch I/S. These tests assess each native contrast separately. A paired uncertainty estimate for D requires additional joint information.

## Replay

With ordinary Python 3 run `python -B analyse.py`, then `python -B compare.py`. Run `python -B verify.py` for independent calculation checks. These scripts use only the standard library and factual aggregate input files. Optional `python -B extract_vectors.py --source-dir PRIVATE` requires PyMuPDF and the two source PDFs named `trehalose.pdf` and `isomaltulose.pdf`. It checks edition hashes. Optional `python -B compare.py --target-figure PATH` regenerates evaluation aggregate inputs from the coordinator’s public `figure.json`. That file fingerprint at extraction was 0d9b19beba9f62387b853db7b0c8dcf4939684fd3a14cdf879ef1fd094235ba3. Coordinates for the two source curves are published as aggregate data. The private source PDFs, renders and text extracts should stay local.

`comparison.csv` supplies per-substitution signed and combined absolute errors and the double-ratio error, `absolute_transfer.csv` every arm forecast, `protocol_change.csv` the matched and no-change results, `raw_ceiling.csv` the incompatibility check. `reading_sensitivity.csv` describes donor perturbations. `forecasts.csv` and `areas.csv` include all alternate timing operators. `sources.csv` identifies sources and figure locators.
