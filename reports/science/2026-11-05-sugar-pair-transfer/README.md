# When do sugar substitution references transfer?

**The data support a time-specific research comparison, but do not select one reduction factor per sugar for INSIGHT.** In a four-drink crossover the two earlier references predicted that replacing glucose with trehalose would reduce the two-hour response much more than replacing sucrose with isomaltulose. The observed fractional reductions were almost the same while fasting. At a linked lunch, isomaltulose had the larger reduction on the signed mean curves. The two-hour participant-positive areas are not printed, but even a conservative bound on the lunch observations rules out an advantage for that fixed forecast over a common reduction. This concerns relative discrimination; it does not establish that any particular common factor is accurate.

### Two substitutions in the same people

Ten adults with impaired glucose tolerance received all four drinks on randomized study days. Let R₁ be trehalose/glucose and R₂ be isomaltulose/sucrose, each a ratio of insulin areas. D = R₁/R₂. Every common multiplier, including equality, predicts D = 1. The frozen pair-specific forecast was **0.527** from previously exposed 25 g trehalose (participant-positive) and 50 g isomaltulose (signed) references. A second forecast came from a separately collected four-drink cohort of ten overweight adults at the same laboratory. Both cohorts received 75 g in the fasted state; each lunch had an individualized sugar serving, linked to the morning assignment. [Target Methods, Fig. 1c,d and Tables 2–3](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0007114511006714) · [Donor cohort linkage and source audit](matched/README.md).

| Signed group-mean area, 0–120 min | Observed R₁ | Observed R₂ | Observed D | Matched donor D | Error: matched / common / earlier pair* |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Fasted drinks | .586 | .601 | .974 | .864 | .120 / **.026** / .614 |
| Linked lunch | .847 | .659 | 1.285 | .862 | .400 / **.251** / .891 |

*Absolute natural-log error on D; lower is better. Independent reading from the publisher GIF with interpolated zero time gave .991 and 1.271. The narrow fasting preference for common over matched is reading-sensitive. Neither a true reversal of a population effect nor independent effects of the two meals follow. At fasting the matched and common geometric-mean references tied on mean component error (.096); at lunch the common reference did better (.126 vs .200). The direct trehalose/isomaltulose area ratio is another prediction: it retains the glucose/sucrose response difference that D cancels. The matched donor predicted 1.524/1.389 at breakfast/lunch vs 1.631/1.495 observed; the cheap constituent reference 1.742 and donor control ratios 1.763/1.611 remain serious alternatives.

**Reach of the native endpoint.** Under nonnegative concentrations, linear individual trajectories between samples and the stated baseline convention, mean participant-positive area cannot be smaller than area above baseline on the mean curve. A concentration-only upper bound needs neither visible error bars nor paired covariance. With ±3 pixels per mean, the lunch sample permits D only within an outer bound of **0.780–1.896** (independent read 0.780–1.890). The entire range lies above the 0.726 equal-error boundary between the earlier forecast and 1, but includes 1. Fasting permits 0.478–2.011 and does not select the closer forecast. These bounds concern the observed sample under the interpolation assumptions; they do not give a confidence interval for a population. Direct arm forecasts on the signed two-hour scale underestimated every target mean: by 30–40% while fasting and 24–45% at lunch. A calculation using measured target control insulin is a separate calibration benchmark. There is no independently varied within-product portion series here.

**Why time remained worth testing.** On the post hoc question of predicting D across 60/120/180 minutes, fasting donor values 1.160/.864/.762 tracked target values 1.240/.974/.765. Mean absolute log error was **.064**, better than even the best single constant chosen with these outcomes (.161). Sol's read gave .082 vs .159 with a different time-zero convention. The advantage is certified for ±0.5 embedded-image pixels at fixed donor coordinates, but an attainable 1-pixel shift per target mean can reverse it. At lunch the matched time series missed at every window (.358 vs .255 for a constant 1; best possible constant .027). The nested windows are correlated; this motivated an outside-laboratory test, not a change in the primary verdict.

### Outside-laboratory prediction: isomaltulose versus sucrose

We froze the target, donor ratios, loss and comparison of **change between windows** before inspecting numerical outcomes in the [Kawai crossover](https://doi.org/10.1507/endocrj1954.32.933). Eight healthy adults each drank nominal 50 g of each sugar in 150 mL water after an overnight fast. Fig. 1 supplies means at 0, 15, 30, 60, 90 and 120 minutes. We retained every published time. The authors’ ΣΔIRI (56.5 vs 118.7 µU/mL) is a sum of concentration increments, not a time-weighted insulin area. No 180-minute forecast was scored. The primary and independent reads of the **signed** isomaltulose/sucrose area ratios were **.391/.460** and **.390/.459** at 60/120 minutes. Neither is a reconstruction of individual positive areas.

| Source of prediction | 60-min ratio | 120-min ratio | Mean error on the two ratios | Forecast growth | Error on growth |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Prior Maastricht time series, 75 g | .415 | .580 | **.145** | 1.398 | .174 |
| Prior Holub time series, 50 g | .494 | .655 | .293 | 1.325 | **.120** |
| Maastricht two-hour ratio kept fixed | .580 | .580 | .312 | 1 | .162 |
| Holub two-hour ratio kept fixed | .655 | .655 | .434 | 1 | .162 |
| Equal grams imply equal areas | 1 | 1 | .857 | 1 | .162 |
| Maastricht one-hour ratio kept fixed (post hoc) | .415 | .415 | .081 | 1 | .162 |

The observed growth was **1.175** (Sol: 1.177). Maastricht predicted the two ratio levels better than Holub, but Holub predicted the change between them better than Maastricht. Both comparisons survived the independent reader’s ±10-pixel perturbations on the native 4,299-pixel-wide scan with scan skew included. Holub beat no change on growth at the central readings; the reading range crosses their equal-error boundary of 1.151, so this last ranking is uncertain. Any fixed ratio between .391 and .460 attains the best post-observation mean error of .081. In particular the donor one-hour constant supplies a counterexample to crediting the improvement over the prespecified two-hour constant to the trajectory alone. It was not selected in advance. A measured-target-60 anchor also needs evaluation physiology and is not an uncalibrated food forecast. [Independent check of the competing profiles](bounds_sol/kawai/HOLUB.md).

The earlier, **exposed** studies show why neither a single constant nor a universal time profile should be selected here. For isomaltulose/sucrose in the same Suraphad volunteers, water and tea gave ratios .382→.553 and .420→.568. Maastricht’s time-specific forecast beat even the respective best fixed constants (.064 vs .184 and .017 vs .151); the two vehicles are not two independent cohorts. In Holub itself the Maastricht curve narrowly beat the optimal constant across three windows (.132 vs .144). In Yoshizane’s trehalose/glucose curves it lost to the simpler 0.420 reference (.429 vs .251). Yoshizane’s separately reported **positive** 120-minute ratio 590/1709, and its stronger evidence against equality than for one particular reduction factor, stand unchanged. [Water and tea](https://doi.org/10.3390/nu9050464) · [Holub](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0007114509993874) · [Yoshizane](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12937-017-0233-x).

**Decision.** Keep the reference’s expected response *at a stated window and under a stated feeding protocol* as a comparator. No clinical correction, portion law or absolute native-area prediction is selected. The next discriminator is a prospectively fixed relative forecast that beats both a cheap fixed magnitude and no change in **growth**, on a separately collected cohort with participant-positive areas at both windows. Four arithmetic arm summaries (two windows × two drinks) plus paired uncertainty for the within-person contrast would connect the time result to the intended endpoint. Linked second meals remain a separate challenge. Decision D stays open.
