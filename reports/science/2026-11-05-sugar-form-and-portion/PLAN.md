# Component identity and portion in simple carbohydrate preparations

Decision. Does distinguishing available glucose and fructose in simple drinks improve predictions of sugar substitution without sacrificing the response to portion size? This is a narrow controlled case of food formulation. Previous wheat/potato, rice, scone and protein findings retain their own domains. No extrapolation from drinks to baked foods.

Selection: Lee & Wolever 1998, PMID 9881888. Its dose design and the existence of the mixture condition are known. The coordinator has not inspected its numerical insulin arm means. Pure glucose and fructose both occur at 25 and 50 g, sucrose and glucose also at 100 g; 100 g of free 1:1 mix is directly tested. Read the endpoint, original arm data, inclusion and baseline method first. Retain a thesis result as such if the published results cannot be reconciled. Each score below concerns ratios of arithmetic arm insulin means, using the verified author operator. Other operators must be reported separately.

Primary portion-and-formulation test: use ONLY the 25 g pure glucose and 25 g pure fructose insulin responses as development anchors. Let R=YF25/YG25. A new ingredient-specific score assigns masses G,F (free carbohydrate equivalents; primary nominal half/half to sucrose; fully report the hydrolysis/molecular-mass sensitivity as described below). Score S=(G+R*F) * (D/25)^(a-1), D=G+F. This keeps a composition mixture homogeneous in dose. Reference score is D*(D/25)^(a-1). Compare both at a=1 and at a learned only from the previously exposed WB1996 native portion study: mean over all four foods of their endpoint log(100/25 response ratio)/log(4); use equal food weights. Also include equality and the glucose-moiety-only forecast (zero for pure fructose, positive coverage separate). Relative forecasts can be anchored at the development cohort YG25 for within-cohort numerical levels; use no held-dose target insulin in these fitted parameters. The no-composition rule at the same exponent isolates formulation information. Sucrose hydrolysis yields ~0.526 g of each monosaccharide per gram of sucrose, so compare sensitivity with both masses times 360/342. The primary convention uses grams ingested and contains no water gain. This hydrolysis choice leaves the sucrose 50-to-100 ratio unchanged.

Primary formulation objective: equally weight the same-dose FREE fructose/glucose and sucrose/glucose comparisons at 50g, and the sucrose/glucose and free-mixture/glucose comparisons at 100g, if available. These share arms. If a cell is unavailable, retain the missing denominator and report coverage; do not silently change the primary panel. Also report 50→100 among formulations directly, absolute levels on held cells, free-mixture versus bonded-sucrose at identical nominal mass, and raw directions. Show whether composition can improve formulation with equal portion performance by construction. Distinguish a forecast relative to a measured low-dose development anchor from food-only absolute prediction.
