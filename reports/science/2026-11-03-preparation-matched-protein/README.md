# Preparation-matched insulin responses under a change of dose and timing

An empirical response reference for **these two hydrolysates** carried more information about their difference than the earlier three-reference mixture or a tie. Pea and soy had the same 18 printed amino-acid percentages in the two reports, for each source separately. Source-matched response data predicted a pea/soy ratio of **1.310**. Protein followed by carbohydrate gave **1.332** for published 150-minute signed area and **1.388** for reconstructed 120-minute signed area. The comparison extends to the *direction* of mean positive two-hour area in the observed sample.

## Source and preparation test

The reference trial gave eight men 0.2 g/kg hydrolysate and 0.2 g/kg maltodextrin together. The second trial gave eight men twice those doses, with maltodextrin 30 minutes after protein. The two trials share a lab; the published demographics differ. Reuse of participants and material lots is undocumented. Identical printed compositions can reflect reuse of a specification or analysis. The direction in the second paper was known when this test was selected. The coordinator recorded the forecast and primary comparison before inspecting the numerical combination outcomes. This is an exposed preparation-specific development test. ## What the sample says about positive two-hour area

The source reports means and SEMs for eight men. With complete measurements of the same men at every time in a given arm, nonnegative insulin, and linear interpolation between scheduled draws, those summaries bound mean area above each man's own fasting level. No correlation is assumed. Across conservative graph-reading allowances of ±0.5 µU/mL for the means and +1.1 for SEM, the empirical positive-area ratio is bounded by **1.074–1.708** (outward rounding). Even the simpler covariance-free bound has a lower ratio above 1.040. The sharper lower difference is 237.1 µU·min/mL. Under an alternative SEM convention raised by Opus, the stressed ratio interval widens to 1.0624–1.7253 and still excludes a tie. These are outer identification bounds for this sample and sampling schedule. They carry no population confidence level. A prediction of 1.310 beats a tie on absolute log error for true ratios above 1.1445. The stressed bounds establish ordering and leave that accuracy comparison unresolved. ## Timing and response levels

The prespecified measured-constituent model added the first component's full area to the second component's area truncated at the protocol's 30-minute delay. At 150 minutes the actual soy-first and carbohydrate-first means were **2654.0 and 3062.4**. The model predicted **2720.5 and 2662.0**. Its two-arm mean absolute error was **233.5**, versus **204.2** for the unscheduled sum and **1523.3** for the first component alone. With carbohydrate-first minus soy-first as sign convention, the contrast was −58.6 predicted and +408.4 observed. The timing criterion failed. Original paired order test *p*=.263. The reference-free order forecast of zero remains a serious comparator.

In the post hoc sampling check, the combined series has no minute-45 draw. That omits the second drink's own minute-15 sample, near the carbohydrate peak. Evaluating the sums on the actual measurement grid moved the 150-minute predicted order difference to +70.9 and the 120-minute difference to +88.5 (observed signed +426.9 at 120). This quantifies sensitivity to sampling. It neither establishes a causal order effect nor calibrates a delay. At 150 minutes the combined-arm graph vs table discrepancies range from −16.1 to +18.5; the single-soy discrepancy was +41.4. Both data streams are retained. [Timing calculations](curves/methods.md)

## Transfer of dose response between intact and hydrolysed protein

Opus implemented a separate, prespecified test with two cohorts of 12, no carbohydrate, and six drinks per cohort. For each target form, calibrate at 0.3 g/kg and forecast 0.4 and 0.6 by multiplying by the opposite form's measured dose ratio. Using the two-hour signed-area bars, this works well for soy and poorly for whey. On eight reciprocal predictions the mean absolute log errors were .275 for the bridge, .219 for proportional dose, .180 for borrowing the opposite form's level, and .410 for no change. For soy the bridge was best at .093; for whey it was .457 vs .151 for the direct reference. This is a cohort-calibrated benchmark. The sampling distribution of the accuracy advantage is unknown. One composition column per source leaves form-specific composition unavailable. The authors' WPH dose test alone gave *p*=.064. [Graded protein study](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0007114507886314)

The original Opus form-ratio section mixed two ways of handling a measured reference. Use the corrected calculation to distinguish dividing two reciprocal forecasts from dividing a single target forecast by its measured partner. The original freeze, calculation, and reviewer objection remain in the packet. This affects interpretation of the form comparison; the eight level predictions and their errors stand.

## Decision and next use

Keep preparation-matched response references as candidates for substitutions with compatible evidence. The successful match does not assign a response to an unrepresented product. It also does not establish a source multiplier across doses, schedules or ordinary meals. The previous non-gluten profile exception, favorable leucine addition result, energy's good fish/rice forecast and earlier gluten and mixture failures retain their original scope. The common-control numerical review at 6262ab9 is closed. Decision D, population-average absolute native positive 0–120-minute area for a specified meal protocol, remains open.

The next discriminator is a case with an external measured reference and at least two composition-matched, specified products tested outside the originating laboratory. Freeze which preparations qualify, include equal-response and energy comparators, and score both the prediction and abstention for unrepresented products. The finite-sample method can recover useful ordering evidence where only a time course with complete-group marginal SEMs is public.
