# When does amino-acid composition improve an insulin forecast?

**The same leucine substitution rule improved one matched-drink prediction and worsened the other two.** A separate trial gave a favorable result when leucine was added on top of carbohydrate and protein in healthy men. These findings support distinguishing *what leucine replaces* from *what it is added to*. They do not establish a general substitution or addition coefficient.

## Changing composition at the same labelled protein and energy

Churchward-Venne et al. randomly assigned 40 young men to five postexercise beverages (eight per group). We selected the four nominal 6.25 g whey arms before reading the bars. Each had 35 g added carbohydrate, 5.68 g added fat, 12.5 g total protein by the authors' table, and 241 kcal. Table 2 gives 6.15 g whey protein for these nominal 6.25 g arms. The fifth arm used nominal 25 g whey and less carbohydrate. It is outside this matched comparison. The subjects performed unilateral resistance exercise before drinking. The reported endpoint is the plasma insulin AUC in Figure 1's inset during the postexercise observation period. The main time course runs to approximately 4.5 hours. We did not establish a separate 0–120-minute participant area or an explicit below-baseline rule for the insulin inset. [Original article, Table 2 and Figure 1](https://doi.org/10.3945/ajcn.113.068775) · [Publisher figure](https://ars.els-cdn.com/content/image/1-s2.0-S0002916523049444-gr1_lrg.jpg)

We developed one substitution coefficient on two exposed half-replacement contrasts from Gunnerud 2012. We regressed the log two-hour sandwich-index ratio on the change in leucine grams, with equality at zero change. The whey and soy training contrasts changed leucine by 0.787 and 0.886 g. The new 2.25 and 4.25 g changes extrapolate beyond both. They also compare other mixtures, cohorts, exercise conditions, area definitions and windows. The forecast used the target drink inputs before we read exact target bar heights. The nonsignificant omnibus result was already known. This is retrospective transfer with no target insulin input. [Training experiment](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0044731) · [Frozen forecast](substitution/frozen.json) · [Evaluation protocol](substitution/protocol.md)

| Relative to W6 with 0.75 g total leucine | Plotted area ratio | Matched-energy / total-grams forecast | Transferred substitution forecast |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 3.0 g total leucine | 1.327 | 1.000 | **1.259** |
| 5.0 g total leucine, added isoleucine and valine | 1.000 | **1.000** | 1.544 |

The primary mean absolute log-ratio error was **0.298** for the transferred rule and **0.103** for equality. Across all six within-family contrasts the errors were 0.312 and 0.146. Training separately on whey or soy preserved the primary disadvantage. Under a ±1-pixel reading tolerance for each bar and the shared zero, the pooled rule's excess primary error stays between 0.146 and 0.213 in the conservative enclosure. The older dose-limited Lan proxy covers only the 3.0 g versus 0.75 g contrast and gives 1.255 there. The clinical omnibus area test across all five conditions gave p = 0.497. The experiment supplies neither equivalence nor a confidence interval on the forecast-error difference. [All predictions, reading bounds and availability](substitution/results.json)

The paper describes the four mixtures as isonitrogenous. **The listed free-amino-acid recipes leave a qualification.** The W6 drink contains 3.18 g added alanine and 3.17 g added glycine. At 5 g total leucine those amounts fall to 1.05 g each, or to 0.03 g each when isoleucine and valine are also added. Assuming the listed free forms, their added nitrogen is approximately 1.09 g in W6, 0.95 g in the 3 g arm, 0.70 g in the high BCAA arm and 0.81 g in the high leucine arm. The whey background is common. The printed gram and energy match is usable. Equal elemental nitrogen is unverified, and the trial varies an amino-acid mixture rather than isolating leucine. ## Adding leucine to an existing drink

Manders et al. served carbohydrate, carbohydrate plus casein hydrolysate, and the same hydrolysate drink with 0.1 g/kg body weight extra leucine. A separate study had reported a 66% increase after adding leucine to glucose. Treating that as a fixed *external empirical multiplier* predicted 1.66 without using any Manders insulin. Other models used the observed carbohydrate and hydrolysate comparator responses and are identified separately. The source dose used fat-free mass, the carbohydrate dose and type differed, and the source window was 150 minutes versus the four-hour Manders area. [Manders et al.](https://doi.org/10.1093/jn/136.5.1294) · [Kalogeropoulou et al.](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.metabol.2008.09.001)

| Added-leucine drink / hydrolysate drink | Observed ratio of mean areas | External multiplier | Protein + amino-acid mass | Energy | No change |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Healthy men, n = 10 | 1.563 | **1.660** | 1.333 | 1.100 | 1.000 |
| Men with type 2 diabetes, n = 10 | 1.259 | 1.660 | **1.333** | 1.100 | 1.000 |

Bold marks the closest of the four forecasts requiring no target insulin. Applying the external increment to *measured target carbohydrate area* gave 1.435 in healthy men and 1.298 in the type 2 diabetes group. The latter is closer than the simple mass forecast but needs two target comparator measurements. The study distinguished the hydrolysate-plus-leucine condition from hydrolysate in healthy men. The corresponding bars in the type 2 diabetes group share a significance letter. No population-by-treatment interaction test or accuracy interval follows. Compounding the paper's reported mean percentage changes gives about 1.93 in healthy men, a different estimand or summary from the prespecified 1.56 ratio of plotted means; the exact reason is unconfirmed. The published source summaries for the same historical leucine response differ. The original abstract gives +66%, later cited as +59%. Reading the original insulin bars gives about +58% directly and +57% after subtracting water from both arms. Water correction alone does not reconcile +66%. Using the alternative source estimates leaves the qualitative healthy/T2D comparison with the simple mass predictor intact. All versions remain in the executable sensitivity. ## Testing two amino-acid partners and the glucose reference

Two Iverson trials gave 25 g glucose and 1 mmol/kg fat-free mass of leucine and a partner. Phenylalanine is heavier than glycine at the same molar dose. Mean leucine doses were 6.98 and 6.89 g, and mean partner doses were 8.79 and 3.94 g. Both trials used fasting-baseline net insulin area over 150 minutes and a Siemens IMMULITE assay. Water was given first and the remaining three conditions were randomized. They share a trial registration and laboratory. Participant overlap is unresolved. The 2014 study explicitly subtracts water for its reported percentages; the 2013 methods do not describe that subtraction. [Iverson 2013](https://doi.org/10.1155/2013/964637) · [Iverson 2014](https://doi.org/10.1155/2014/521941)

| Mixture/glucose response or forecast | Leucine + phenylalanine | Leucine + glycine |
|---|---:|---:|
| Published ratio | 1.77 | 1.24 |
| Food-energy approximation | 1.631 | 1.433 |
| Lan total amino-acid mass | 1.333 | 1.256 |
| External empirical protein-panel constant | 1.244 | 1.244 |
| Sum of historical AA-plus-glucose increments | 1.73 | 1.60 |
| Measured pair and glucose from the target cohort, one water background | 1.413 | 1.100 |

The historical sums are close on phenylalanine and overpredict on glycine. They use earlier human insulin measurements on the individual ingredients with glucose, from separate samples. The historical glycine study followed participants for two hours. The historical leucine summary also changes from 1.66 to 1.59 across the two papers. The *same-cohort* benchmark instead adds the response to the amino-acid pair alone to glucose, counting water once. It is below both observed mixtures. For phenylalanine the measured forecast was read from the bars on the fasting-baseline scale. For glycine the published water-adjusted pair-alone response was already 10% of glucose. Among the prespecified rules, the food-energy estimate had the smallest two-study average level error (0.113); the measured pair benchmark had the smallest error on the between-study relative contrast (0.106). The authors' outcomes were exposed. Two related experiments cannot establish a universal winner. [Forecasts, alternatives and source definitions](pairs/results.json) · [Plan and exposure](pairs/protocol.md)

**The denominators change the scientific interpretation.** On a common fasting-baseline scale the plotted mixture means are approximately **2,136 and 2,125 µU·min/mL**. The respective glucose-only means are **1,212 and 1,714**. The ratio of the two normalized ratios is 1.422, equal to the ratio of mixture means (1.005) times the inverse ratio of glucose means (1.414). Subtracting water in both studies gives a corresponding decomposition of 1.410 = 1.011 × 1.394, to rounding. Under ±1-pixel reading choices the raw mixture-mean ratio ranges from 0.975 to 1.037; the inverse glucose-mean ratio ranges from 1.351 to 1.481. These are technical reading bounds. This comparison supplies no randomized partner swap, no fraction causally explained by glucose, and no equivalence of population mixture means.

A post hoc check illustrates the prediction consequence. Copying the phenylalanine study's measured mixture level into the glycine study gives 2,136 against 2,125 observed. Importing the phenylalanine study's mixture/glucose ratio and calibrating it to the glycine study's measured glucose level gives 3,021. Both are external measured-response benchmarks. Their apparent accuracy depends on this particular pair. Detailed native, normalized and water-adjusted results are retained separately.

## Decision and reproduction

Keep simple ingredient-mass and energy references. The protein-source offset at the previous checkpoint failed transfer. Here, an external leucine multiplier works in one healthy addition protocol, and a transferred substitution slope misses both high-leucine point means in a matched postexercise protocol. These results support recording the whole substitution and reference protocol for research. They do not justify a required leucine field, a consumer multiplier or native positive two-hour validation. The favorable earlier 90-minute component result and its cheaper energy comparator remain intact. [Prior checkpoint](https://github.com/parthganguly/insight-insulin-app/tree/035315fd8c94d030df1091ece2d59f19ee131aea/reports/science/2026-11-02-protein-source-transfer)

The next discriminating observation is a common-protocol comparison of both amino-acid partners in the same participants, with their mixture means and the common carbohydrate control reported. Alternatively, a substitution series with comparable carbohydrate and explicit replacement ingredients could test whether a full-profile forecast improves on the matched tie. Record the native area and the reference area separately before judging normalized contrast accuracy.
