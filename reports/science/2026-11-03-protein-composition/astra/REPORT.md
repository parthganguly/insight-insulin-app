# Free leucine added to carbohydrate and casein hydrolysate

In ten healthy men, adding free leucine to the same carbohydrate and casein hydrolysate increased the mean four-hour insulin area by approximately **56%**. A frozen forecast of **66%**, taken directly from a separate leucine-plus-glucose trial, came closer to the observed increase than the forecasts based on ingredient mass, energy or no change. In ten men with type 2 diabetes in the same experiment the estimated increase was **26%**, and the 66% forecast performed worse than all three simpler comparisons. The two populations are reported separately.

| Forecast for carbohydrate + hydrolysate + leucine / carbohydrate + hydrolysate | Healthy forecast | Healthy absolute log error | T2D forecast | T2D absolute log error |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| No change | 1.000 | 0.447 | 1.000 | 0.230 |
| Macro-derived energy | 1.100 | 0.351 | 1.100 | 0.135 |
| Protein and free amino-acid mass | 1.333 | 0.159 | 1.333 | 0.058 |
| Published external leucine multiplier | 1.660 | 0.060 | 1.660 | 0.277 |
| Mass applied to observed hydrolysate increment | 1.114 | 0.339 | 1.183 | 0.062 |
| External 0.66 increment applied to observed carbohydrate area | 1.435 | 0.085 | 1.298 | 0.030 |

The final two rows use observed comparator areas from the target cohort. Their different forecasts expose a concrete modeling choice. A 66% increment measured with carbohydrate can be carried either as a multiplier on the whole new mixture or as an addition proportional only to the carbohydrate response. The former is closer for the healthy men and the latter for the T2D group. The first four rows require no target insulin input. The model does not forecast absolute insulin areas from a food record. RESULTS.json also reports carbohydrate-only and energy comparisons for H/G and HL/G, and the explicitly control-calibrated HL/G forecasts.

**Meals and outcome.** All three 4 mL/kg boluses contained 0.7 g/kg body weight carbohydrate, split equally between glucose and maltodextrin. The two protein drinks included 0.3 g/kg enzymatically hydrolyzed sodium caseinate. The third also contained 0.1 g/kg free leucine. At the healthy group's mean 83.7 kg, these correspond to about 58.6 g carbohydrate, 25.1 g hydrolysate and 8.37 g free leucine. Addition changes both nitrogenous mass and energy. The energy scenario uses 4 kcal/g for the specified nutrients and omits any contribution from the common flavor mixture. The protein-mass comparator simply scales whole response with added nitrogenous ingredient mass; the separate conditional comparator scales only the observed hydrolysate-associated increment. Neither estimates elemental nitrogen mass. Blood sampling continued to 240 minutes. Methods describe areas above baseline. [Manders et al., original paper](https://doi.org/10.1093/jn/136.5.1294) · [Author thesis, chapter 3](https://cris.maastrichtuniversity.nl/ws/files/1082519/guid-78f42af4-bc96-44c0-bcb6-cb707e48e0c4-ASSET1.0.pdf)

**Transfer assumption.** Kalogeropoulou et al. reported a 66% increase in serum insulin area when leucine was given with 25 g glucose in 13 healthy participants. Their protocol used 1 mmol of leucine per kg lean body mass and 150 minutes of observation. Manders used body weight, a mixed carbohydrate, casein hydrolysate and 240 minutes of observation. The 1.66 is a rounded empirical ratio carried across these conditions. Comparable ingredient identity supports testing it, while the distinct doses and horizons limit what one favorable forecast establishes. [Kalogeropoulou et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19013300/)

**Reading method.** We read the Figure 1B bars against axis ticks at 0 and 120, not Figure 1A concentration curves. The publisher raster is 475×707 pixels. EVIDENCE.json records the bar tops, ticks, pixel uncertainties, source letters and prose percentages. Estimated mean areas (G, H, HL) were about 41, 63 and 98 in healthy men and 24, 53 and 66 in T2D, in the source's four-hour area units. The 32 combinations of ±2-pixel bar-top and ±1-pixel axis uncertainty gave HL/H ranges 1.526–1.601 and 1.222–1.297 respectively. An independent second reading gave approximately 1.58 and 1.26. These ranges describe graphic reading precision. Figure 1B and the source comparison distinguish healthy HL and H at p<0.05. T2D H and HL share the same significance letter. No group-by-treatment or model-error significance is inferred.

The source text also reports H and HL increases against G of 66±20% and 221±82% in healthy controls and 141±40% and 204±37% in T2D (mean ± SEM as reported). Compounding the displayed healthy percentages gives 3.21/1.66 ≈ 1.93, appreciably different from the observed mean-bar ratio of 1.56. The source does not explicitly reconcile the aggregation. The bar means govern the frozen ratio-of-means endpoint; the percent summaries and their SEMs cannot supply a paired CI for that ratio. [Publisher Figure 1](https://ars.els-cdn.com/content/image/1-s2.0-S002231662208244X-gr1_lrg.jpg)

The healthy result supports retaining ingredient identity as a candidate alongside simple mass and energy. Because the study lacks an equal-energy, equal-mass addition of another free amino acid on the same background, it cannot identify leucine's specific effect versus that comparator. This is an operational four-hour result. No direct claim about the two-hour endpoint or a personal response follows.
