# Johansen et al. 2024 extraction receipt

The dose-by-product comparison was specified before extraction. The main study included three separate double-masked crossover cohorts. Healthy volunteers received 50 g each of oligomalt and maltodextrin plus an unrelated 15 g inulin condition (15 randomized and completed). Participants with overweight or obesity received 33 g and 50 g of each carbohydrate (26 randomized, 24 completing all four periods). Participants with type 2 diabetes received 50 g of each carbohydrate (22 randomized, 20 completing both periods). Supplementary Table S3 divides the overlapping overweight/obesity cohort by BMI, 13 per stratum at randomization. Between 33 and 50 g, only the overweight/obesity cohort supplies a within-study within-person design. Model estimates include available observations. The historical 33 g healthy-volunteer study cited by the authors is distinct.

Both ingredients were given as a single dose in 300 mL of water, after at least 10 h fasting, consumed within 10 min, with approximately 1 week between periods. Methods specify the same ingredient gram dose and expressly describe the comparator as isocaloric. They supply no measured metabolizable energy per arm in kcal, or separate analytical available-carbohydrate mass. Oligomalt average degree of polymerization 16.5; comparator Glucidex 40 DE glucose syrup, average DP 3. Oligomalt contained a small proportion of free sugars including leucrose. These are two products differing in several structural features. Nestlé Health Science funded this exploratory study. Blood sampled at baseline, 15, 30, 45, 60, 90, 120, 150, 180 min. Insulin is secondary to glucose.

## Three distinct insulin estimands

- Main Figure 2 and Supplement Figure S5 display numerical, adjusted relative differences in insulin iAUC (60, 120 and 180 minutes) from the mixed model. The author describes percent contrasts as the model treatment difference divided by estimated comparator mean. The CSV converts percent reductions to ratios via 1 − reduction/100. The model includes product, period, sequence and a participant random effect nested in sequence. Published p-values test the product comparison at each dose. The paper gives no paired uncertainty or interaction p-value for between-dose changes. The subgroups are nested within the PwO cohort.
- Table 3 reports participant-level ratios for 2 h, termed insulin index, maltodextrin set to 100. In cohort order HV50, PwO33, PwO50, T2D50 the arithmetic mean (SD) indices are 44 (14), 44 (16), 50 (24), and 63 (44). Table 3 also provides Cmax and Tmax. These are different estimands.
- Native-unit arm iAUC means were **not tabulated** in the article or supplement. Neither registry had posted outcome results on inspection. Native-unit estimates in the CSV are manual reconstructions from published aggregate trajectories in Figures 2 and S5. Use them as approximate baseline-subtracted net areas, expressed in µIU·min/mL. The linear trapezoid on group mean trajectories differs from a fitted repeated-period model and can also differ from averaging participant-level positive-area iAUCs. Pixels are rounded marker centers. The reproducible CSV stores manual y coordinates and calibration, actual units and times. Marker overlap and raster precision limit accuracy, especially at fasting and late times. Scripts do not access participant records.

In the four-period dose crossover the adjusted OM/MD ratio at 0–60 min was .401 (33 g) versus .424 (50 g); the corresponding descriptive integrated-mean-curve ratios instead were .427 vs .406. At 2 and 3 h both estimands support a higher ratio at 50 g for the full cohort. The BMI 25–29.9 subgroup has the opposite adjusted trend at every window compared with the BMI ≥30 subgroup. No significance of a dose-by-product interaction can be inferred from these four directions or the product-versus-comparator p-values.
