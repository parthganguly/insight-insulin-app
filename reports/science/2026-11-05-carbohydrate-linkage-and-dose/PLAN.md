# Can the linkage between the same two sugars predict an insulin substitution?

Decision. At equal served carbohydrate, sucrose and isomaltulose each yield one glucose and one fructose on complete hydrolysis, so the accepted constituent rule predicts equal response for any common fructose weight and portion exponent. Test whether distinguishing the two linkages predicts the magnitude of a substitution across protocols, and whether a change in the response's timing adds value beyond a fixed response ratio. This is a narrow family of preparations. The intended endpoint remains population-average native participant-positive two-hour insulin area. Signed mean-curve area, published three-hour areas and post-lunch/pre-fed epochs are different targets. Keep results study by study. Do not reconstruct a participant-positive endpoint from a mean trajectory.

Selection before numerical extraction by the coordinator: use Holub et al. PMC2943747 trial 2, fasted healthy adults, 50 g isomaltulose and sucrose in water. Its qualitative lowering and delayed glucose are known. Do not mix this study with its ileostomy or biscuit cohorts. Test on the fasting challenge in van Can 2009 (PMID19671200), its separately labeled second mixed-meal challenge, and Keesing 2019 (PMC6893461) 50 g following lunch. The coordinator has already seen the published 2492/609 result for Keesing in a prior search; its exact summation convention is to be checked. The external investigators may have read outcomes when acquiring papers. This is donor-only fitting with separately collected tests under known outcome exposure, not prospective validation. Additional first mixed meals require actual insulin data; missing studies are not successes or failures. Disclose overlapping cohorts, ingredient background, periods and fasting baseline. No pooling across studies. No author outreach.

Define s(t) and i(t) as sucrose and isomaltulose concentrations minus each arm's pre-challenge baseline at its own time zero. Linearly interpolate between actual samples; integrate the resulting lines with breakpoints, including transformed breakpoints when the clock changes. Use no tail extrapolation. Fit on Holub using all the observed increments from >0 to 180 min, equal point weights, least squares in native concentration units. A common multiplier in the units cancels within this fitting. Compare four maps from s to the iso curve: equality; amplitude c*s(t) with one fitted nonnegative c; unit-area stretch s(t/k)/k with one k; amplitude plus stretch c*s(t/k)/k with c>=0. For k use the fixed inclusive grid 1 to 4 by 0.001; for each k the optimal c is the nonnegative least-squares projection (one parameter for amplitude, two jointly). Pick smallest k on a tie. The interval expresses a hypothesized slower clock and is a search boundary, not evidence of physiological kinetics. Also retain the simpler empirical donor signed I120/S120 ratio applied as a scalar (same two-hour endpoint, one development contrast). Its time profile if used is constant scaling. Source-only benchmark uses the corresponding Holub pair of arm means or signed areas unchanged per 50g, with a separately declared linear dose multiplier only when actual served dose and unit bridge are documented. Dose alone predicts an unchanged same-dose ratio. A ratio reference requiring only food identity on transfer differs from curve transforms evaluated using target sucrose insulin. Neither produces uncalibrated food-only absolute insulin.

Primary comparison per evaluable protocol: absolute log error on 0–120 iso/sucrose ratio of signed integrals of the two displayed mean trajectories. Publish native signed areas (separately) for all arms. For empirical reference vs equality the target sucrose measurement is unnecessary to predict the ratio. For time transformations applied to the target sucrose curve, mark that input as measured. An unsupported signed or nonpositive ratio is unavailable for log loss, not clipped. Score 0–60 separately for early magnitude; 60–120 and 120–180 as diagnostic signed area changes with no logarithm when nonpositive. Evaluate published individual positive iAUC by exact window, if linked, separately including native ratios/direction/paired or marginal uncertainties; label any bridge from signed estimates as conditional. Keep any whole-curve diagnostic distinct from primary ratio error. Keep portion behavior visible: a shared linear multiplier cancels for equal-dose ratios, and separate cohorts at different doses cannot identify a within-food dose slope. Do not use study/arm insulin outcomes to back-fill missing portions or tune parameters after evaluation. If reliable curves identify systematic representation failure, test a focused, explicitly post-result restriction or a better independently evaluated hypothesis instead of searching another coefficient on the test means.
