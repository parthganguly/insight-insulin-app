# What the carbohydrate is made of helped predict a substitution at two portions

A reference for two named glucose polymers predicted their two-hour insulin-area ratio at a larger serving. In Johansen's four-period crossover, oligomalt gave **0.420** of the maltodextrin response at 33 g. Carrying 0.420 forward predicted the observed **0.450** at 50 g much more closely than counting equal carbohydrate grams (1.000). In a separate 25 g trehalose/glucose study, the same reference predicted 0.420 against **0.345** on the intended above-fasting endpoint. This is evidence for a relative forecast for specified products. Absolute insulin areas and the change with serving size require separate tests. [Johansen, Figure 2](https://doi.org/10.3390/metabo14080410) · [Yoshizane, Table 1](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12937-017-0233-x)

## A direct test on positive two-hour insulin area

Twenty participants completed the fasted trehalose/glucose crossover. The investigators gave 25 g of each sugar, using 28 g of a trehalose dihydrate product to deliver the stated 25 g. They excluded area below each fasting concentration. Mean serum insulin areas over 0–120 minutes were **590 ± 101** for trehalose and **1,709 ± 142** for glucose (means ± SE). The ratio of those means is **0.345**. The authors separately report a **0.36** mean of within-person ratios. Two of 24 participants had diarrhea after trehalose and were excluded. Outcomes for those participants are unavailable. [Source methods, Table 1 and Discussion](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5292800/) · [Opus implementation](opus_trehalose/REPORT.md)

| Frozen forecast for trehalose / glucose | Predicted ratio | Absolute log error against 0.345 |
|---|---:|---:|
| Equal grams | 1.000 | 1.064 |
| Glucose released on complete hydrolysis, using anhydrous masses | 1.053 | 1.115 |
| Two-hour isomaltulose / sucrose reference | 0.655 | 0.640 |
| Scalar fitted to that same pair of insulin curves | 0.521 | 0.411 |
| 33 g oligomalt / maltodextrin reference | 0.420 | 0.196 |

The 0.420 forecast came closest among these candidates. Every candidate overpredicted the ratio. The publication gives paired tests and marginal standard errors but omits the paired covariance. For a positive ratio r, the standard error of the paired contrast T − rG is at most SE(T) + r SE(G). Inverting a two-sided paired-t comparison under its assumptions gives a conservative approximate 95% interval of **0.189–0.568** for the ratio of population means in the analysed group. Even allowing for integer rounding expands it only to 0.188–0.569. The equal-error midpoint for 0.420 and equality is 0.648, above the whole interval. The midpoint for 0.420 and 0.521 is 0.468, inside it. The data therefore distinguish the transported reduction from equality more securely than they distinguish the two smaller forecasts. This post-result uncertainty calculation treats the predictions as fixed. It does not include uncertainty from re-estimating donor references. **Absolute prediction gave a different answer.** Lee's separately measured 25 g glucose response of 8.1 nmol·min/L gives a reference of 1,350 µIU·min/mL under the usual 6.00 conversion. Applying 0.420 forecasts **567** for trehalose, close to 590, while the glucose reference misses 1,709 by **359**. Using the evaluation study's measured glucose instead forecasts **718** for trehalose. The close food-input level thus partly reflects offsetting errors. At the historical 6.945 assay convention, 0.521 comes closer on the trehalose level. Ratios retain their ranking. These exposed level checks assume minutes in the integration. Yoshizane prints concentration times “2 h.” Literal hour integration would permit at most 68.7 for glucose and 22.5 for trehalose from the reported mean within-person peaks and baselines. Assay comparability remains uncertain. ## What changed with the portion

Only Johansen's group with overweight or obesity received both products at both doses (26 randomized, 24 completing all four visits). Each product came in 300 mL water after fasting. Equal nominal ingredient doses and the authors' isocaloric designation establish the simple comparator. The study did not measure absorbed grams for each serving. Oligomalt and the DE40 comparator differ in bond structure, polymer length and minor sugars. [Products and trial methods](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11356256/) · [Study extraction](receipts/johansen.md)

| Two-hour insulin measure | Oligomalt | Maltodextrin |
|---|---:|---:|
| Approximate signed group-curve area at 33 g (µIU·min/mL) | 1,645 | 4,105 |
| Approximate signed group-curve area at 50 g | 2,509 | 5,595 |
| Observed 50 g / 33 g | 1.525 | 1.363 |
| Proportional forecast from each measured 33 g arm | 2,493 | 6,220 |
| Previously learned sublinear forecast from each measured 33 g arm | 2,273 | 5,672 |

Proportional growth (1.515) came closer for oligomalt. The prior solid-food exponent of 0.778 (growth 1.382) came closer for maltodextrin. Both beat carrying the low-dose level forward unchanged. Sol's separate graph reading gives ratios of 1.529 and 1.383 and the same ordering of forecasts. The portion models need a measured low-dose response to supply these absolute numbers. A constant formulation factor preserves their portion forecasts algebraically. The model that predicts both products from grams alone misses the substitution even when its prediction for maltodextrin is close. [Every level and portion forecast](results.json) · [Independent portion reconstruction](receipts/sol_mixed.md)

The source's **adjusted** two-hour ratios are 0.420 and 0.450. Its mean individual two-hour indices are 0.44 and 0.50. Signed group-curve integration gave about 0.401 and 0.448. Each statistic answers a different question. Johansen specifies the two-hour window but omits the clipping algorithm for iAUC. Its p-values test products within a dose. No paired uncertainty for the change in product contrast across doses was recovered. In the two exploratory BMI strata, the adjusted ratio moved in opposite directions (0.470 → 0.391 and 0.385 → 0.482). Transferring the dose adjustment learned in either stratum worsened the other's high-dose forecast relative to keeping its own low-dose ratio. That last comparison is outcome-exposed. [Original supplement, Figure S5](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/metabolites/metabolites-14-00410/article_deploy/metabolites-14-00410-s001.zip)

The healthy cohort supplied a separate 50 g ratio of **0.413**, near the other cohort's 0.420 and 0.450. In the type 2 diabetes cohort the 50 g ratio was **0.522**. There the generic scalar (0.521) was closer than the product reference. Carrying both healthy 50 g signed areas to the overweight/obesity cohort underpredicted both high-dose arms by about 861 and 1,501 µIU·min/mL. These cohorts share a manufacturer-funded report. The data support keeping a specific reference alongside the cheaper general comparators. They give little basis for assigning every product a permanent dose exponent. ## Where a more elaborate prediction lost

Sucrose and isomaltulose yield the same constituents when fully hydrolysed. Holub's 50 g drink curves supplied the scalar and two-hour reference above. A fitted time stretch and amplitude reproduced the donor pattern, but using the evaluation cohort's measured sucrose curve gave variable results. In van Can's fasting test the fitted scalar was closest of the frozen clock alternatives. At the linked second lunch the pure time stretch did better than the joint fit. Keesing's post-lunch mean isomaltulose increments fell below the starting concentration. The positive ratio required for log scoring was unavailable on that signed curve, while the study's reported iAUCs were positive. See the [clock forecasts](results.json) and [source and window reconciliation](RECONCILIATION.md).

| First or linked meal | Observed signed two-hour iso/sucrose ratio | Entire-meal reference | Diluted by share of carbohydrate replaced | Diluted and weighted by named sugars |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Suklaew, 40 g sugar of 67 g breakfast carbohydrate | 0.621 | 0.655 | 0.794 | 0.841 |
| Zhang, 50 g sugar plus 24 g meal carbohydrate | 0.729 | 0.655 | 0.767 | 0.812 |
| van Can, lunch with 25% energy from sugar and 30% from other carbohydrate | 0.797 | 0.655 | 0.843 | 0.888 |

Equality predicts 1.000 throughout. Simple dilution improved the first prediction for Zhang and van Can but worsened it for Suklaew. Adding the prior fructose weight worsened all three relative to simple dilution. Thus the earlier muffin gain from named sugars survives, and it gives no general license to multiply a second correction into a mixed meal. In Suraphad's same-participant water and tea arms, both source readings favored the simple drink reference over equality, while the bar and prose readings disagreed about the vehicle difference. [Thai breakfast](https://digital.car.chula.ac.th/chulaetd/19253/) · [Zhang, Figure 5](https://doi.org/10.3390/nu17152539) · [van Can trial](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0007114509990687) · [Water and tea trial](https://doi.org/10.3390/nu9050464)

**Decision D remains open.** For a fully specified substitution, retain product identity and compare an external product reference with equal grams and a general slow-carbohydrate reference. INSIGHT could obtain identity from a verified recipe or ingredient label and a documented reference table. A photo or a total-carbohydrate entry alone cannot distinguish these pairs. The strongest next test is whether the trehalose- and isomaltulose-specific references forecast *different* contrasts in the same separately collected cohort. Van Can's impaired-glucose-tolerance study tested both sugar pairs and provides a concrete opportunity to check that discrimination under fasting and meal protocols. Any reported longer-window or signed outcome must be scored separately. A direct matched timed-release assay for the Johansen preparations remains unavailable.
