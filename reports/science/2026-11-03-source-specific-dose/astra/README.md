# Two controlled wheat-preparation studies: extraction and challenge
The postexercise crossover is a preparation-by-dose experiment, not a botanical source comparison. On average wheat hydrolysate alone showed little change on doubling, whereas the half-wheat, quarter-free-leucine, quarter-free-phenylalanine preparation increased. The latter replaced part of the hydrolysate at matched grams of added mixture; it did not simply add free amino acids on top of matched wheat. This demonstrates a conditional preparation-specific pattern. It does not validate a transferable coefficient.

## Sources and design
- Source calibration: [van Loon et al., AJCN 2000, primary PDF](https://cris.maastrichtuniversity.nl/ws/files/526718/guid-3a70aa35-87d0-4a49-b3ba-2f588d60238f-ASSET1.0.pdf), DOI 10.1093/ajcn/72.1.96. Eight healthy nonobese men resting over two hours, 0.8 g carbohydrate/kg/h (half glucose, half maltodextrin). Arm 1 carbohydrate control; #7 0.4 g wheat hydrolysate/kg/h; #9 0.2 g wheat hydrolysate plus 0.1 g each free leucine and phenylalanine/kg/h. Arms 9 and 10 were devised after testing 1–8; randomization was within stages. It is not a fully randomized 7-vs-9 comparison. At 0–120 min text reports +54% wheat and +42% blend vs control, neither significant. At 60–120 min it reports +103% enriched; the authors focused on the second hour after observing early variability. Training has only one positive dose per preparation, so no curvature is identified.
- Target: [van Loon et al., J Nutr 2000, primary PDF](https://cris.maastrichtuniversity.nl/ws/files/1219768/guid-aea863c5-0aab-42d8-94a7-889f59e38628-ASSET1.0.pdf), DOI 10.1093/jn/130.10.2508. Eight trained male cyclists after glycogen-depleting exercise; crossover, random order, 30-min feeds, t=0 after exercise and before feeding, CHO 1.2 g/kg/h and formulations above at 0.2 and 0.4 g total additive/kg/h, for 3h. Two-factor ANOVA used treatment and subject; Tukey contrasts among arms, not a formal composition-by-dose interaction. The authors report +52% and +107% above carbohydrate-only for enriched low and high, high 36% above low; wheat-only arms did not differ significantly from control. Absence of significance is not equivalence.
- Published means/SE only. Calibration and target are different experiments by the same group; participant overlap unverified. Context includes group, exercise, carbohydrate amount/composition, timing, and possible preparation period; their contributions cannot be separated.

## Interpretation
Cross-study transfer of the resting 0–120 min preparation ordering fails even though target's within-protocol pattern is pronounced. In the later window calibration blend exceeds plain, but the plain response still misses substantially; a descriptive sign match does not establish slope transfer. Statistical significance for one dose contrast and not the other is not a test of their difference. Without participant-level paired covariance, this extraction supports no formal interaction p-value. Highly engineered drinks with free amino acids during postexercise recovery must not be extrapolated to meals without further independent data. Outcomes seen during search and source selection are not an untouched prospective holdout. No biological coefficients should be set from these results.
