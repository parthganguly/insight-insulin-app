# Which food did the lentils replace?

**Two human trials support a conditional prediction. Replacing potato with lentils reduced the reported two-hour insulin area more, proportionally, than replacing rice with lentils.** The older trial predicted that difference in a later set of recipes. It underestimated the reduction in both recipes. Accounting for the fraction of the meal’s carbohydrate exchanged improved the four point forecasts, although the assumption connecting exchange fraction to insulin response remains unvalidated.

## A prediction carried between trials

Both teams served a control and substitutions supplying 25 g available carbohydrate from small green or split red lentils. Moravek tested plain rice or instant potato meals supplying 50 g total available carbohydrate. Chamoun tested rice chili and potato soup; the whole control meals contained 27.3 and 26.9 g available carbohydrate. The lentil meals ranged from 25.3 to 26.6 g. Table values below are lentil/control ratios of geometric mean capillary plasma insulin iAUC from 0 to 120 min. A ratio of 0.63 means the lentil preparation had 63% of its control's reported response. Both studies sampled seven times; neither article explicitly describes clipping below baseline. We compared four ways to transfer the earlier human measurements without using a later insulin measurement as input. Keeping the displaced starch gave a predicted potato/rice **ratio of the substitution ratios of .698** for both lentils. The later values were **.569 and .719**. Pooling all earlier measurements, or using color alone, predicted 1.000. Matching both starch and color gave .723 and .674. Knowing the displaced staple conveyed information about the contrast. The two recipes also differ in ingredients, preparation and background nutrient amounts, so the contrast cannot isolate the causal contribution of starch identity.

**The declared primary comparison failed.** On the four later substitution ratios, predicting no change gave mean absolute log error .678; pooling the earlier effects, matching the starch, matching the color and matching both independently gave **.397 each**. All four models predicted smaller reductions than observed in all four meals. As a result, their absolute log errors equal their signed log errors; regrouping the same four donor log ratios preserves the average. The better potato/rice contrast and the tied four-meal accuracy are compatible. Proportional available carbohydrate scored .639 and the earlier fitted carbohydrate–protein–fat equation .668 on the same four. An unscaled direct transfer of the six native geometric means had MAE 6.03 nmol·min/L. Wheat muffin had no matched wheat donor; large green had no matching later intervention. **A candidate prediction for a specified serving.** The two studies exchanged the same 25 g, but that accounted for half the carbohydrate in the plain meals and about 92%–93% in the chili and soup controls. We multiplied each donor log ratio by the later exchange fraction divided by 0.50. This retains the original ratio at half replacement and gives a ratio of one at zero replacement. It assumes a log-linear relationship with the *share of control carbohydrate exchanged*. The study did not measure that relationship within a fixed recipe. Using the starch-specific donor under this assumption reduced four-meal error to **.157**, versus **.231** using the same scaling on the pooled donor. The improvement was concentrated in potato soup. The unscaled .698 forecast remained closer on the potato/rice contrast overall (log error .117 versus .225 scaled). Reverse transfer favored scaled starch matching (.085 versus .183 for scaled pooling), but uses the same studies. The central rice estimates still overpredict the later insulin ratios. No paired confidence interval for the model advantage is recoverable from the marginal arm intervals alone.

This extension was chosen after results from the later trial, the older rice values and the qualitative older potato result were known. It was evaluated without fitting an exponent. It is an outcome-exposed model-development result. The two trial registrations differ, but the investigations shared a research unit, three authors and funding. Eleven participants completed both older starch experiments (37 unique); 42 people completed the later studies, with 22 in at least two. Cross-publication overlap is unknown. The earlier insulin assay was ALPCO, the later one Mercodia. The papers report geometric means and confidence intervals. They do not supply the arithmetic mean of participant-positive two-hour areas. The later authors also report relative insulin response percentages from a different calculation, for example 46% and 45% for soup versus geometric-mean ratios 39% and 42%. The investigation does not substitute one for the other.

## What transferred between recipes without an older matched food

Within the later study, we learned a common log substitution ratio from two recipe families and forecast both lentil meals in the third, repeating for all three. Across six substitutions its error was .395 versus .508 for equality, .491 for proportional available carbohydrate, .498 for fitted carbohydrate and .511 for fitted carbohydrate–protein–fat. The common reference improved chili and soup but overpredicted the muffin reduction; it predicted .508 against .830/.863. Learning separate green and red references gave .404 across six and increased error for red/green contrasts from .091 (tie) to .147. Lentil identity as an intervention carried more information here than color refinement. Five of the six comparisons differed significantly from their control in the source; the red muffin was the exception and neither lentil significantly differed from the other within recipe. Whole-recipe holdouts prevent using the held recipe's outcomes in training but do not make the participants independent. ## A separate native forecast for six postexercise foods

Haigh and colleagues served resistance-trained adults egg whites, whole egg, pork, salmon, lentils or mycoprotein, each at 0.25 g protein/kg, after exercise. The original supplement gives assayed recipes, portions and separate early and late insulin estimates. Before seeing the six numerical arm areas, we froze the older Wolever equations trained on nine food/dose rows with positive two-hour areas. The sample's early omnibus P=.805 was already known. The following transfer uses the declared mean-body-mass portions, a midpoint for whole-egg carbohydrate reported below 0.1 g, and the stated conversion of 6 pmol/L per µIU/mL. The training target was fingerstick plasma under another eating protocol. The comparison is to the study’s directly reported **early area panel**, on its own operator and assay. The native joint criterion failed. The six reported means in food order were approximately 698, 533, 525, 784, 680 and 678 mIU·min/L (SEM 104–210). The three-nutrient model forecast 1057, 721, 836, 739, 1581 and 995. Salmon was a favorable individual result. The joint failure survives using actual served grams × per-100-g composition, the reference 80 kg portion, whole-egg carbohydrate limits or the alternative 6.945 conversion. The latter changes native MAEs substantially (e.g. 253 for the three-nutrient model versus 174 for carbohydrate and 190 for the training mean); it has no effect on the ratio metric. Six parallel arm estimates generate 15 dependent contrasts. Insulin was tertiary, and the trial was designed around protein synthesis. P=.805 does not establish physiological equality. ### The published insulin windows cannot be combined as written

The six early areas come directly from Supplementary Figure 2a. The late panel has negative means for pork and salmon, ruling out an exclusively positive-area operator there. In lentils the early and late estimates are about 680 and 802, whereas main Figure 2b shows a total of about 1110. The six totals minus window sums were +148, +91, +85, +137, **−372**, +117. A positive-only total could not fall below its signed-window sum if the participants, baseline, window and integration grid were common. This difference exceeds the observed graph-reading resolution. It does not identify the cause.

Methods specify t=0; the legend refers to postabsorptive values. Insulin was already elevated at meal start after exercise. A signed trapezoid using t=0 on the plotted mean trajectories fails to recover the early panel. A −30-minute draw reproduces five of six main totals closely and most late estimates; it does not reproduce the early panel. A separate nonincremental-area figure is much closer to integrating −30 to 300 than 0 to 300. The source also reports some interpolation of missing samples. These diagnostics do not establish the authors' actual operator or authorize replacing their reported means. For this study alone the minimal useful clarification is a common set of six arm means for 0–120, calculated above **each participant's** meal-start baseline with the crossing rule and n, plus the baseline/visit set used in the late and total panels. For Katsanos, the previously identified arm summary remains unavailable. ## Decision

Retain the displaced ingredient and fraction of available carbohydrate exchanged as **research inputs for specified substitutions**. The harder test is another existing recipe or exchange level where the fraction-scaled and fixed-reference forecasts diverge, scored against a declared endpoint and without refitting. For example, at a 75% exchange (25 of 33⅓ g available carbohydrate), the fraction-scaled ratios from these donors would be about .859 for rice and .501 for potato; the fixed references would stay at .904 and .631. Absolute prediction of the population-average participant-positive two-hour response remains open. This research result changes no consumer score. [Execution and contributions](RECONCILIATION.md)
