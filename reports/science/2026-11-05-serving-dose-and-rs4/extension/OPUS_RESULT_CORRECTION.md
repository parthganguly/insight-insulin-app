1. **Donor operator.** Al-Tamimi 2010 states a trapezoidal iAUC (Prism) without an explicit negative-area convention. The donor is therefore *not* known to be participant-positive. The frozen inputs already said "below-baseline handling unspecified". RESULT.md "Limits" should state the consequence explicitly: the donor factor may come from a different area operator than the target's.
2. **Unequal arm counts.** The target has unequal arm counts: Figure 2 shows n = 37/36/37, with 38 evaluable, and the per-arm iAUC n is not printed. Means come from partly different participant sets, and no paired covariance is reported.
3. **Withdrawn: "Neither dose-only nor generic enrichment is supported over the other … statistically indistinguishable."** That wording compared a 0.07-log difference in mean model error against the marginal ratio SE (about 0.15). That SE is not an uncertainty interval on differences in average model error. Replacement:
   - **Central order** of the primary mean |ln(pred/obs)|: available CHO 0.403 < equality 0.419 < generic enriched 0.470 < graded 1.580. High/medium: available CHO = generic 0.061 < equality 0.072 < graded 0.692.
   - **Bracketing.** The observed ratios (0.682, 0.635) lie between the available-CHO forecasts (0.990, 0.979) and the generic forecasts (0.413, 0.409). The models err in opposite directions, so their small error difference says only where the observations fall between the two forecasts. It does not show the models are equivalent, and none is endorsed.
   - **Uncertainty preserved.** The observed-ratio marginal log SE scenarios stay as reported: medium about 0.15 at ρ = 0, range 0.016–0.218 over ρ. The donor band at ρ = 0 is unchanged. Each describes the uncertainty of one ratio, not of an error difference.
4. **Decision relevance, last bullet.** Replace "On these data the two are statistically indistinguishable" with: "Available CHO is centrally first and generic third. The observations are bracketed by the two forecasts, so no equivalence or superiority is claimed. The graded rule is rejected."
