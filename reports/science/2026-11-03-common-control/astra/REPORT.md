# What Quek's amino-acid table can predict per serving

**Strict coverage is zero for the four food additions.** The paper measures the fat and protein in the test meals and reports amino acids in milligrams per 100 g of each source food. It gives weights of finished mixed meals, but does not identify the matched-basis weight of fish, boiled egg white, steamed taukwa or chicken used in the amino-acid assays and consumed by each participant. Each addition also included a teaspoon of light soy sauce. Multiplying a source-food concentration by whole meal weight would include rice as though it were the source food. [Quek et al., Tables 1–2 and preparation methods](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0007114515005498)

We kept the previously fitted log-ratio slopes fixed and examined two explicitly conditional conversions. First, assuming the leucine share of measured total amino acids applies to the 25 g additional Kjeldahl protein yields 2.045, 2.055, 1.985 and 2.050 g leucine for fish, egg, soy and chicken. Second, assigning the entire difference in served meal mass to matched-basis source food yields 2.179, 2.724, 1.373 and 1.984 g. The latter also implies 33.1 g total assayed amino acids for the egg addition versus 25 g reported added protein. These are chemically different measures, but the discrepancy illustrates why this cannot establish a served dose. No external database could identify the unreported cooking yields or sauce retention more directly.

| Conditional input basis | Equal-protein tie | Frozen pooled slope | Frozen whey slope | Frozen soy slope |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Source leucine / total AA × 25 g | 0.1660 | 0.1628 | 0.1644 | 0.1616 |
| Source concentration × serving-mass difference | 0.1660 | 0.1529 | 0.1383 | 0.1675 |

Entries are mean absolute log errors across all six dependent source-pair ratios within one cohort. Pooled-slope error was slightly lower than a tie under either assumption, yet both conversions assign more leucine to egg than fish, whereas the observed fish/egg area ratio was about 1.27. The two dose rules also change which separately trained slope is closest. These results provide a sensitivity analysis of the missing denominator, not validation of any served-dose coefficient. Energy and fat are not matched across arms. The slopes were developed from changes in amino-acid formulation on a matched background, so they do not yield an absolute level or a complete addition-versus-rice prediction.

**Original outcome.** Fifteen healthy Chinese men ate all five rice meals. Table 1 reports 50 g available carbohydrate throughout and protein of 4.5 g in plain rice versus 29.5 g in each addition. Figure 2B reports group mean insulin incremental area to 120 minutes. Methods specify trapezoids excluding area below baseline for glucose and the same treatment for insulin; the caption nevertheless calls it net incremental area. Preserve that terminology discrepancy. The text prints rice 2606.5 and soy 2860.6. The five rectangle heights in Figure 2B on PDF page 4 (area tick span 4500 over 121.222 local PDF points) recover rice 2606.5, fish 3935.5, egg 3090.5, soy 2860.9 and chicken 2970.9. Use the chart-derived group means consistently for ratios; negligible deviations from printed values reflect graphical precision. The original authors report that both fish and egg increased the area compared with rice (p<0.02). They do not supply the paired difference variance for every one of our six protein-source contrasts. Their treatment finding should not be substituted by the meta-analysis's assumed-correlation CI for egg versus rice.
