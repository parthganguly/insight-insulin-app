# Post hoc energy comparator

Table 1 reports 50 g available carbohydrate in every meal and 29.5 g protein in each of the four protein meals. Fat differs, especially for soy beancurd (7.6 g versus 0.4–1.4 g for the other additions). Applying 4 kcal/g to reported carbohydrate and protein and 9 kcal/g to fat gives estimated meal energies of 220.7 kcal for rice, 327.0 for fish, 321.6 for egg white, 386.4 for soy and 330.6 for chicken. Energy is derived from the table, not directly measured. Methods identify Kjeldahl and Soxhlet analyses and describe carbohydrate as available; Table 1 labels the row carbohydrates.

Across the **six pairs of protein meals**, proportional energy produced mean absolute log-ratio error **0.2541**, above the matched-carbohydrate/total-protein tie (0.1660) and all conditional leucine estimates (0.1383–0.1675). Fat is the only input that changes the energy prediction in this comparison. It makes beancurd the highest-energy meal, while the beancurd insulin-area point mean was the lowest among the four. Energy improved on the tie for fish versus egg and worsened the other five pair errors. This result concerns the specific proportional energy rule; it cannot establish that fat is uninformative or that either imputed leucine dose is correct.

Against **plain rice** the ratio-of-mean insulin areas were 1.510 for fish, 1.186 egg, 1.098 soy and 1.140 chicken. Energy predicted 1.482, 1.457, 1.751 and 1.498, respectively. Mean absolute log error was 0.2413 for energy, 0.2016 for the constant carbohydrate ratio and 1.6787 for proportional total protein. Total protein predicts 29.5/4.5 for every addition, a very different benchmark from its exact tie within the four enriched meals. The actual reference was rice alone (printed area 2606.5), not an oral glucose drink. The source-variable plot label omits the time dimension although the underlying endpoint is 120-minute integrated insulin area. Only dimensionless ratios are scored.
