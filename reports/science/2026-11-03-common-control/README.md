# Can an amino-acid profile transfer between meals with a common control?

A model that used 16 amino-acid measurements to select and combine three external hydrolysate responses **lost to predicting equal responses** across six separately tested hydrolysates. It helped on eight of fifteen formulation contrasts. All five comparisons involving gluten got worse. A different leucine-based rule did help distinguish an arginine-only drink from a drink with both leucine and phenylalanine. These results answer different questions about composition.

## Frozen transfer to a separate two-hour crossover

The development library was the two-hour insulin response to whey, pea and wheat hydrolysates in eight men. Each received the same carbohydrate schedule. The test cohort comprised eight healthy men receiving pea, rice, soy, gluten, whey and egg hydrolysates, each with maltodextrin, and maltodextrin alone. Every test drink provided 0.2 g/kg of each of hydrolysate and carbohydrate, except the carbohydrate-only control. Development used repeated drinks and a 0.5 protein-source-to-carbohydrate mass ratio. Evaluation used a single bolus and a ratio of 1. The work came from separate publications with an overlapping research setting. [van Loon et al., Tables 1–2 and Figure 5](https://doi.org/10.1093/ajcn/72.1.96) · [Claessens et al.](https://doi.org/10.1038/sj.ejcn.1602896) · [Original author's thesis, chapter 2, Table 2.1 and Figure 2.2B](https://cris.maastrichtuniversity.nl/ws/files/777184/guid-9f242a19-cd77-461e-b12c-d451b47ab051-ASSET1.0)

We combined aspartate with asparagine and glutamate with glutamine to match the reported assay groups. We omitted lysine and tryptophan from **all** profiles because some development values were missing. We expressed the remaining 16 entries as fractions of their shared total. The model approximates each test profile with the nearest weighted average of the three development profiles, using nonnegative weights summing to one. It then applies those weights to the development mixture/control insulin ratios. Only food composition enters at prediction time. The empirical responses used for development come from human measurements. We make no assumption that the inferred weights are physical ingredient proportions. The same-source profiles differed by only 0.18–0.23 percentage points in total variation on the normalized coordinates. Lot identity, common assay calibration and effects of hydrolysis are unverified. The target reports 9–27% hydrolysis across preparations. **Formulation differences.** Mean absolute log error on all fifteen pairs was 0.255 for the composition model, 0.205 for a tie and 0.259 for a nearest-profile lookup. On the three nominally corresponding sources, copying source-specific ratios gave 0.377 against 0.246 for the tie. The profile model predicted gluten/whey at 1.107; the reported net-area means give 0.691. On the ten pairs excluding gluten, its error fell to 0.155 versus 0.192 for the tie. That exclusion is a post-result diagnostic. Reassigning all six frozen predictions to the six labels gave lower pair error for 350 of the 720 assignments. The actual assignment ranked 351st. **Levels and availability.** The model generated six predictions. All six profiles lay outside the exact triangle spanned by the three development profiles; the computed weights are approximations. The highest ratio any convex weighting can produce is **1.591**. Even granting the most favorable training reading gives **1.758**, below the lowest admissible target net-area ratio, **1.868**. Changing the weights cannot close that gap. Six-arm mean absolute log error was 0.662 for the model, 0.596 for the training mean, 0.229 for a macro-energy ratio of two and 0.918 for no change. Doubling the external increment to reflect the doubled protein-to-carbohydrate ratio improved the model's level error to 0.478 but worsened its contrast error to 0.337. The unnormalized-profile sensitivity and all local-control-calibrated area predictions are also recorded. A native level forecast based on the latter requires the target cohort's measured control insulin. [Complete results](profiles/results.json)

**Connection to positive two-hour area.** For the same samples and integration convention, participant positive area equals signed net area plus the magnitude of area below fasting. Averaging preserves that identity. To reverse the central observed whey/gluten ordering on a positive-area endpoint, gluten's mean below-fasting area would have to exceed whey's by about **815** native plot units. To reproduce the model's exact ratio, gluten's missing area minus 1.107 times whey's would have to be about **1,096**. For all six positive-area ratios even to fit within the model's central ceiling, the carbohydrate control's below-fasting area would have to be at least **795** in the same units. These are requirements on missing measurements, not estimates of them. The smallest additional summaries for the ordering question are the two arm means of below-fasting area and their paired contrast uncertainty. The control summary is additionally needed for comparisons relative to maltodextrin. [Executable endpoint calculation](profiles/diagnose.py)

## What did work in the ten-drink study?

Opus tested all ten drinks against a common carbohydrate control in eight men. They drank at 0, 30, 60 and 90 minutes. For drinks 3 and 9, the source already disclosed unusually large second-hour effects, and the authors formulated drinks 9 and 10 after analyzing drinks 1–8. The 0–120-minute area above the initial baseline is native two-hour evidence for that **repeated-feeding protocol**. The 60–120-minute area is a distinct supporting endpoint. The initial numerical graph reads were made by Opus and subsequently checked by Sol. The source does not fully document the individual below-baseline clipping rule. The displayed units have a possible hour/minute scaling ambiguity, so no absolute conversion was used. [Primary study](https://doi.org/10.1093/ajcn/72.1.96) · [Endpoint correction](public/vanloon/CORRECTION_v2.md)

- On the second-hour area, the control was about 18 in source units. The arginine-only drink gave 14; the arginine/leucine/phenylalanine drink gave 36.5. A **capped** leucine proxy reduced nine-arm mean absolute log error to 0.182 versus 0.239 for macro-energy. Removing arginine alone left a smaller point advantage over energy, whose sign could change with the graph reading. The original domain-limited proxy covered only five of nine additions. Capping and the linear tail are explicit extensions. [All forecasts](public/vanloon/RESULTS.json) · [Common-reading comparisons and coverage](public/vanloon/CORRECTION_v2.json)
- Across the six protein-source pairs, the separately trained substitution slope had error 0.255 versus 0.228 for a tie in the second hour, and 0.275 versus 0.233 over 0–120 minutes, using the per-50-g-carbohydrate basis. The earlier 1.66 **addition** multiplier was inapplicable here. [Six-pair reconciliation](public/vanloon/PAIRS_v3.md)
- Drink 10 was a nominal half blend of drinks 3 and 7. On the full two-hour window, the parent means were about 52.0 and 52.5, the linear measured-parent prediction was 52.25, and the observed blend was 47.0 (control 33.0). The blend stayed below both parents under the declared reading ranges in that window. The analogous second-hour ranges overlapped. Using full-dose measured parents assumes linear mixing; the trial had no individual half-dose arms for that calculation. No paired interval for this model residual was reported. The text/figure discrepancy for the full-window mean of drink 9 remains explicit. [Clinical readings](public/vanloon/outcomes.json) · [Versioned interpretation](public/vanloon/CORRECTION_v2.md)

## A prepared meal closer to the consumer question

In Quek's crossover, rice alone gave a two-hour insulin area of about **2,606**. Rice with fish gave **3,936**, egg white **3,091**, taukwa **2,861** and chicken **2,971** on the source's scale. The Methods apply a below-baseline-excluding trapezoid to insulin, though the figure caption calls it net. Fish and egg each increased area relative to rice in the authors' repeated-measures test. [Original methods, Tables 1–2 and Figure 2B](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0007114515005498)

All four meals added about 25 g protein, but Table 2 reports amino acids per 100 g of the source food, and Table 1 weighs the whole rice meal. The paper does not establish a matched-basis serving weight for each assayed food. Thus **zero of four exact serving-leucine predictions are available**. Scaling leucine by its fraction of reported total amino acids and 25 g added protein gave exploratory six-pair error 0.163 versus 0.166 for the equal-protein tie. Subtracting cooked rice weight from whole-meal weight instead gave 0.153. Both scenarios ordered egg above fish, while measured fish/egg was about 1.27. These outcomes and the original figure were exposed before running the scenarios. Neither scenario makes an unmeasured serving an observed input. In a post-result check, proportional whole-meal energy had higher six-pair error (0.254) than the tie (0.166). It did well on fish versus rice but ranked the higher-fat taukwa meal first among the four, whereas taukwa had the lowest insulin area. [Astra's source admission and all six comparisons](astra/REPORT.md)

Decision D remains open. For composition to justify a consumer adjustment, it must improve formulation contrasts in a new, preparation-matched collection and supply the required food inputs. For native level prediction, that collection must report the control and each meal's participant-level positive two-hour area under the specified eating protocol. Accurate profile matching alone satisfied neither condition here.
