# External preparation comparison

## Moro et al., 2019

In a Texas crossover experiment, ten young men consumed intact or hydrolysed whey protein at 0.08 g protein/kg in approximately 300 mL water after an overnight fast. Participants received both drinks in randomized double-blind order, 4–6 weeks apart. The study used infusions of labelled leucine and phenylalanine, forearm infusion and heated-hand arterialized sampling catheters, femoral venous sampling and serial muscle biopsies. The investigators enriched the drinks with the labelled amino acids (7.5%). The primary outcomes concerned muscle metabolism. Meiji supported the study. These are independent participants and experiment relative to Claessens. The trial lies 0.22 g/kg below the smallest training dose. Use the 0.3 g/kg estimate only as a boundary extension. The article explicitly gives both preparation categories and protein dose. A supplement contains composition details, but its download returned HTML in this audit. The degree of hydrolysis and detailed composition are unverified. [Original study and Figure 4](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7443767/#fig4)

Panel D shows serum insulin measured by ELISA (the results prose also uses “plasma”). The axis is µU/mL. Basal and 15, 30, 45, 60, 90, 120, 150 and 180 minutes appear at uniform horizontal spacing despite unequal times. Symbol centers in the native 661 × 1991 image were read on a 2× zoom. Open circles and dotted lines are intact, filled circles and solid lines are WPH. Basal markers nearly overlap. Point estimates and ±SE bars represent n=10. Stars at 15 and 30 indicate change from basal. The authors report no significant between-drink difference. [Original figure](https://cdn.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/blobs/ac8c/7443767/9f4c7c1b4183/nxz053fig4.jpg)

## Replay and comparisons

Run `python replay.py`. The inputs in figure.json are native image pixel coordinates, and training.json records published training group means from Claessens' experiment. The units-per-pixel calibration uses the 0 and 15 ticks. Subtract each arm's own baseline, count areas below baseline as negative, and use trapezoids at the actual labelled times. AUC is in µU·min/mL. These are ratios of group mean areas, not averages of participant ratios. The 120-minute analysis is the only endpoint matched in duration to training. The 180-minute result is descriptive window sensitivity. [Training experiment](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0007114507886314)

The three predictions are respectively 1, 1.001864 and 1.358921. Intact whey reaches a higher plotted 15–30-minute peak, then falls further below its own baseline. Subtracting baseline over 180 minutes amplifies the difference. Changing the integration horizon materially alters the inferred ratio.

The ±2 native pixel rectangular perturbation applied to all extracted marker centers gives a 120-minute ratio range of 1.005–1.629 and a 180-minute range of 1.326–3.975. It is a chosen reading scenario, not a confidence interval. Translation or positive scaling of the common vertical calibration cancels from this net-area ratio. Signs of trapezoidal weights give exact extrema of the chosen box. At the lower 120-minute endpoint equality is closer than the boundary rule. Sampling uncertainty is larger than these data can resolve: individual paired serum values and within-person covariance over times and drinks are unavailable, so a paired AUC confidence interval and significance of error differences cannot be recovered. Do not interpret either ranking as a validated correction. Marker readings are from one observer; independent re-reading is desirable.

## Morifuji et al., 2010

The published abstract identifies ten participants split across soy and whey trials (five each), exposure to hydrolysed and nonhydrolysed protein independent of carbohydrate, and greater insulin stimulation following whey hydrolysate. It supplies no insulin timepoints, areas, participant weights or numerical means. ACS labels the article available to purchase. The Waseda author-affiliation portal exposes the abstract. The Meiji research listing cites the paper without figures. ResearchGate offers request-only access. OpenAlex lists no PDF URL, including for the institutional metadata entry. No eligible quantitative endpoint was verified. The soy within-source insulin rank is likewise unresolved from the primary abstract. [Primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20614926/) · [ACS](https://doi.org/10.1021/jf101912n) · [Waseda](https://waseda.elsevierpure.com/en/publications/comparison-of-different-sources-and-degrees-of-hydrolysis-of-diet/) · [Meiji bibliography](https://www.meiji.com/global/food/food-development/common/pdf/library/Sports_Nutrition_Research.pdf)

A position stand reports 12.5 g per treatment. This is secondary dose evidence. Conditional on that serving, the Claessens cohort means of 72.2 kg (soy) and 79.8 kg (whey) imply 0.173 and 0.157 g/kg respectively, making 0.3 the nearest training dose in both cases. With actual target mean body weight unknown, any body weight over 41.67 kg would put that serving below support; 35.71 kg or greater selects the 0.3 boundary, allowing a tie at the threshold. Conditional soy and whey boundary predictions are 0.781 and 1.359. All-dose constants are 0.801 and 1.002. None can be scored against the original's qualitative abstract. [Secondary dose lead](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12970-017-0177-8)
