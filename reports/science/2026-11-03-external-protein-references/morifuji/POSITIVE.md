# Post hoc individual positive-area bounds at 120 minutes

The endpoint is the sample mean of the area above each participant's own fasting serum insulin, integrating straight lines through draws at 0, 15, 30, 45, 60, 90 and 120 minutes. Units are µU·min/mL. The signed net-area comparison in RESULT.md remains the frozen comparison. This addendum examines what the published group summaries can establish about a different endpoint. [Primary Figure 4D](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7443767/)

Figure 4's caption specifies means ±SE for ten participants. We assume the same ten contribute at every plotted time and conventional empirical SEMs. The trial reports a crossover, but paired concentrations and within-person covariance are unavailable. Positive areas computed from the central arm mean curves are 250.89 (WPH) and 269.68 (intact). This yields a descriptive ratio of 0.9303, whereas the central signed ratio was 1.2750. Early intact concentrations peak higher and later fall further below baseline. Clipping a group-mean curve gives a lower bound by Jensen's inequality, not the mean of the individual positive areas.

These rounded-outward outer bounds do not establish either sample ordering. For absolute log error the boundary prediction 1.358921 beats equality only for observed ratio above sqrt(1.358921), approximately 1.1657; the corresponding threshold versus geometric prediction 1.001864 is approximately 1.1668. The bounds span both thresholds. Containment does not prove the extreme values are jointly attainable. There is no population confidence coverage or inference of treatment efficacy.

## Calculation and checks

Run `python positive_bound.py` beside figure.json. It uses the retained finite-sample method with n=10. For concentrations X0 and Xt and D=Xt−X0, the empirical standard deviation with divisor 10 equals three times the conventional SEM. The positive-part upper bound is the least of the later concentration mean (individual basal and later concentrations are nonnegative), the Cauchy bound (mean(D)+hypot(mean(D),sd0+sdt))/2, and a finite-count bound. For each possible fraction f=k/10 with negative D, the count bound takes the smaller of (1−f)mean(D)+sqrt(f(1−f))(sd0+sdt) and mean(Xt)−(1−f)mean(X0)+sqrt(f(1−f))sd0, then maximizes across counts. Between draws, integrate straight lines joining upper values by convexity. For the lower bound integrate the positive part of the interpolated mean change exactly, including crossings. Baseline change is identically zero. Cross-arm covariance is unnecessary for the reported sample-mean outer bounds.

The input file records mean marker centers. The finite-bound script separately records conservative minimum and maximum cap y-coordinates pooled across both marks at a given draw. It does not assign overlapped caps to arms. The 30-minute extent is treated as unavailable. At the other draws the optional caps analysis assumes each represented SEM is no longer than the distance to the most distant visible cap of either arm, allowing ±2 px each on caps and centers in the stress scenario. When a cap is unavailable, an ordinary SEM for n nonnegative observations cannot exceed its sample mean (the maximum occurs when one observation carries the whole sum). The cap-free check uses only that bound and the concentration means everywhere. Separate high or low later and basal means give conservative upper or lower envelopes. The ±2 px scenario conditions on the recorded tick calibration and is not a statistical confidence interval. The agreement of the two stressed envelopes shows that the public drawing's ambiguous caps supply no additional separation under these conservative assumptions.
