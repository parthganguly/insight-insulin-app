## Design and measured response

Healthy adult volunteers were randomized to one recipe within age stratum in parallel, with no within-person crossover. Older participants were recruited from an aging registry and young participants through newspaper advertisements. In 26%, 41% order, older n=10 each (3F/7M, 5F/5M) and mean ages 66.7, 66.5 years. Young n=8 each (4F/4M both) and ages 30.6, 28.8. Table 1 gives these enrolled group sizes, without a separate insulin-completer count. Both groups received EAAs as a bolus in 250 mL noncaloric noncaffeinated drink after an overnight fast. A stable-isotope phenylalanine tracer (~9% of unlabeled phenylalanine) was also in the bolus. Table 2 reports both mixtures as 6.726 g. The higher recipe substituted leucine for seven other amino acids. It raised leucine from 1.721 to 2.790 g while isoleucine, valine, histidine, lysine, methionine, phenylalanine and threonine all fell. The paper prints no tryptophan row. The printed component entries sum to 6.727 and 6.725 g owing to rounding.

Arterial plasma insulin was measured by ELISA (reported CV 1.8%). The authors used a group × time repeated-measures ANOVA and Tukey comparisons after significant F tests where appropriate. They reported a significant insulin time effect (P<0.05), no significant group effect, and increases over basal through 30 minutes in the young groups and 45 minutes in the older groups. No numerical group P, interaction P, equivalence margin, or recipe-specific insulin area test was reported. The text describes peaks appearing at 15 minutes in young and 30 minutes in older adults. The 41% young vector plot has its almost-tied highest marker at 30 minutes. The analysis integrates the full curve. This is an isolated fasting EAA challenge measured in arterial plasma, with no whole-food reference or directly measured insulin secretion rate.
